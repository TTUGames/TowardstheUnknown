# Map

## Generation

`Assets/Prefabs/LevelDesign/Map.prefab` holds the `Map` component and the exit VFX; each scene uses a variant of it from `LevelDesign/Maps` that adds its generation. `Map` asks its `MapGeneration` component for a grid of `RoomInfo` and the spawn position:

- `RandomMapGeneration` picks room prefabs from a `RoomSet` asset (`Assets/Data/Rooms`, listing the rooms of each type), within a max size, with a number of treasure and combat rooms and a distance to the boss room. Combat rooms carry several enemy layouts (in their [layout](#room-layouts)) tagged with a difficulty; the generator splits `totalDifficulty` across the combat rooms, each between a min and a max, and `CombatRoomPool` picks a room and layout of each difficulty, reusing them only once every layout of that difficulty is used. `CheckValues` refuses settings a map cannot meet, including a difficulty between the min and the max that no combat room layout has. The placement is random and can run out of free cells: `Generate` retries with a fresh layout up to 100 times, then logs an error and goes on with the last, incomplete, layout. `verbose` logs the difficulties and the grid of `RoomType`s (`PrintMapLayout`, a letter per type; `UNDEFINED` and `EMPTY` are the generator's own cells). The rooms are then filled breadth first from the spawn, the neighbours shuffled (`ListShuffler`), each with a random room of its type and, but for the combat rooms, a random layout index.
- `FixedMapGeneration` places hand-made layouts.
- `GalleryMapGeneration` lays every room of a `RoomSet` in a row, west to east, without enemies (layout -1): the spawn rooms first, the boss rooms, a dead end, last (`includeBossRooms`). The `RoomGallery` test scene walks through the game's set.

`RoomInfo` remembers whether a room was visited, its layout index and the room itself once loaded. The index (`Room.LayoutCount`) counts the enemy layouts of the room's `RoomLayout` first, then its `SpawnLayout` components (`TreasureSpawnLayout`, a `LevelDesign/TreasureSpawnLayout.prefab` spawning its `TreasureSpawnPoint`s); `FixedMapGeneration` names them by that index. The treasure rooms, `SpawnRoom1` and `Antechamber` hold one: the last two have no enemy layout, and a room drawn by type (`GenericRoomPool`) without any layout throws. So the spawn room and the antechamber give a chest too (see [chests](inventory.md#chests)).

## Rooms

Every room prefab is a variant of `LevelDesign/Room.prefab` (through `CombatRoom.prefab` or `TreasureRoom.prefab` for those types), which holds the shared settings such as the tile model list. `RoomSet` assets and `FixedMapGeneration` reference the rooms' `Room` component, so converting a room into a variant requires remapping those references. Room types: `SPAWN`, `COMBAT`, `TREASURE`, `ANTECHAMBER`, `BOSS`.

Entering a room (`Map.EnterRoom`):

1. `RoomInfo.LoadRoom` instantiates it on the first visit and gives it the materials of the current [edition](editions.md), then `Room.SetExits` removes the exits leading nowhere and adds the exit VFX on the others; on the next visits it reactivates the room kept from the previous one, with its loot and random tiles as they were. `Room.Init` registers the player in the turn system, spawns the layout (first visit only; each enemy of an enemy layout is placed on the top of its cell's tile, plus its offset, and gets the edition's materials too), warms up the VFX of its enemies, of the player's artifacts and the entities' hit VFX (see [Combat](combat.md#vfx)) and raises `GameEvents.RoomEntered`.
2. The room's `PlayerDeploy` places the player: next to the entrance by default, on the spawn tile for `SpawnPlayerDeploy`, on a deploy tile of the player's choice for `CombatPlayerDeploy` when enemies are present (the tiles of the layout's deploy cells, the first one by default; a room with enemies and no deploy cell, or a deploy cell without a tile, throws with the room's name), and without enemies next to the entrance, or on the first deploy cell when no exit matches the side the player comes from (the boss room entered from the gallery) (the HUD's action button ends the deploy phase).
3. `TurnSystem.CheckForCombatStart` starts the combat, or the exploration. The room locks its exits during a combat, spawns its reward when it ends (`Room.SpawnReward`: the first `TreasureSpawnPoint` of the room, in the combat rooms the `LevelDesign/RewardSpawnPoint.prefab` of `CombatRoom.prefab`, drawing from an `ArtifactPool`, often empty), and opens its exits.

Stopping on an exit (`TransitionTile`) out of combat raises `GameEvents.RoomLeft`, covers the screen (`Hud.Fade`, a `SlantedWipe`), deactivates the room (kept for a next visit: `Tile` only lists the tiles of the active rooms) and enters the adjacent one. The minimap (`MinimapPanel`) follows the current room.

### Room hierarchy

Every room lays out its objects the same way, edition first, each in an empty group at the room's origin (position, rotation and scale 0/0/1):

```
CombatRoom3
├─ Board         Floor, Exits, Void, Walls: the tiles by terrain, Walls also the Wall.prefab colliders
├─ Shared        what both editions show: Rocks, Props, Crystals, Nature, Fences, Lights, Torches, Water…
├─ Anniversary   listed by the root's EditionOnly Anniversary: Grass, Nature, Lanterns, Water, Volumes…
├─ Classic       listed by the root's EditionOnly Classic: Lamps…
└─ SpawnLayouts  the reward and treasure spawn points (CombatRoom.prefab, TreasureRoom.prefab)
```

`Board` and `Shared` come from `Room.prefab`; a room has `Anniversary` and `Classic` only when it shows something in that edition only, and no empty group. The categories are `Rocks` (rocks, cliffs, stairs), `Props`, `Crystals`, `Nature` (the `Environment/Nature` prefabs: trees, vines, roots, mushrooms, plants, water lilies), `Fences` (the fences' models; their colliders are `Wall.prefab`s in `Board/Walls`), `Lights`, `Lamps`, `Lanterns`, `Torches`, `Water` (surfaces and drips), `Grass`, `Volumes`, `FX`; a group that must stay whole (a group a component references, such as the phases of `DraregArena`, or an inactive one) goes in `Groups`. The objects are named after their source prefab or mesh, without the pack's codes, numbered across the room by group and position (`Rock_01`, `LightCrystal_02`), the tiles after their cell (`Tile_03_06`). A component never sits on a group (the tool refuses one).

**Tools > Level Design > Tidy Room** (`RoomTidy`, `Scripts/Editor`) lays out the selected room prefabs so (or the one open in Prefab Mode, once closed), their bases first; **Tidy Room (Report)** lists what it would do and saves nothing. It files each object by explicit rules (its components, its source prefab's folder and name, the `EditionOnly` of the root listing it or a parent, the terrain `RoomLayoutBaker.ClassifyAll` gives a tile), keeps its world transform, renames it, removes the containers left empty and makes the root's `EditionOnly` list its edition's group instead of the objects. It saves a room only if each edition shows the same components at the same places (`RoomTidy.Fingerprint`, the `EditionOnly` simulated) and a dry run bake of its layout changes nothing, and a second run does nothing. A variant can't move nor rename its base's objects: the tool reports them, to tidy in the base. Tidy a room after adding decor to it.

## Room layouts

A room's gameplay layer is a `RoomLayout` asset (`Assets/Data/Rooms/Layouts`, one per room, named after it), referenced by its `Room` and shown in its inspector: a 15 x 15 grid (`RoomLayout.Size`, the largest rooms fill it; cell (0, 0) is the south west corner, x going east and y north), placed in the room by `origin` (the room's local position of cell (0, 0)'s tile). Each cell is `Empty`, `Floor`, `Hole` (not walkable, the line of sight passes: an invisible tile at the floor's height), `Wall` (not walkable, blocks the line of sight: an invisible tile `wallHeight` times taller) or `Exit` (a walkable tile with a `TransitionTile`, its direction in `exits`). The layout also holds the deploy cells and the enemy layouts (a name, a difficulty and enemies: a prefab, a cell and an offset from the tile's top, for an entity standing between cells such as Drareg). `Room.TileAt(cell)` finds a cell's tile at runtime.

The terrain lives in the tiles: the layout's inspector (`RoomLayoutEditor`, an Odin editor, `Scripts/Editor`) paints it and **Bake terrain into the room** (`RoomLayoutBaker.Bake`) writes it into the rooms using the layout, in Prefab Mode with undo when the room is open there. The bake only touches the cells whose terrain changed: a new tile is a `Tile_x_y` `Tile.prefab` instance copying a floor tile of the room (scale, mesh, material), a changed tile goes into the `Board` group of its terrain (`Floor`, `Exits`, `Void` or `Walls`, see [room hierarchy](#room-hierarchy)), a removed cell loses its tile; the other tiles stay as they are, so a bake of an unchanged layout changes nothing. The decor is not baked: the rocks of a hole or the model of a wall are placed by hand, as before. **Import from the room** reads the terrain and the exits back from the tiles after a hand edit (`RoomLayoutBaker.Classify`: an unwalkable tile is a wall when it is taller than the floor, in a wall group or under a collider such as a fence, a hole otherwise), centering the room in the grid and moving the deploy cells and the enemies along. Tools > Level Design > Create Missing Room Layouts gives a new room prefab its layout, imported from its tiles. The deploy cells and the enemies are read at runtime, so they need no bake.

`RoomLayoutBaker.Bake(root, layout, dryRun: true)` only counts the tiles a bake would add, remove or change.

The inspector's brushes: Floor, Hole, Wall, Exit (its direction follows the side of the room it is on), Erase, Deploy (toggles a deploy cell; they are numbered, the first is the default) and Enemy (places the chosen enemy prefab, from `Prefabs/Entities` with an `EntityData`, in the selected enemy layout, drawn with its timeline icon; the enemy list starts with the enemies placed the most across the layouts; layouts are added, duplicated and deleted there, their tabs showing their name and difficulty). Left click or drag paints, right click erases; the Deploy and Enemy brushes act on the click only, a drag does nothing. A legend above the grid gives the cells' colors and marks (○ a hole, ■ a wall, an arrow an exit). It warns about deploy cells and enemies off the walkable cells, enemies on deploy cells, empty enemy layouts and two exits in one direction.

## Tiles

Tiles are instances of `Assets/Prefabs/LevelDesign/Tile.prefab`, which nests the selection overlay (`TileOverlay`) and references it from a serialized field; per-tile mesh, material, collider, decoration children and `TransitionTile` are overrides. Build new rooms from this prefab, never from raw models with a `Tile` component added. `LevelDesign/Wall.prefab` is a fence (LowPolyCavePack's `ZLPC_Fence_A_06` with a box collider) placed in `CombatRoom3`, `CombatRoom9` and `CombatRoom13`. The exits' sides are a `Direction` (`NORTH`, `SOUTH`, `EAST`, `WEST`), turned into grid vectors and opposites by `DirectionConverter`. A room gives its plain tiles a random model and rotation from its list at load.

- `Tile` components form the grid: neighbours are found by physics overlap in `Awake` and stored in `lAdjacent`, keyed by world direction. A tile holds at most one entity. `Tile.BoardVersion` changes each time an entity or a collectable takes or leaves a tile (`SetEntity`, `Collectable`): what depends on who stands where caches on it (the enemies' threat).
- A tile's `Selection` (movement, attack, deploy) and `IsTarget` flags drive its overlay; `Tile.ResetTiles` clears them. `Room.Update` sets the hovered tile's `IsTarget` when it isn't already: every paint goes through `Tile.Paint`, which keeps a target tile's. `Tile.BlinkRefused` blinks its overlay twice in the threat material (real time), its paint waiting meanwhile: `PlayerTurn.RefuseClick` calls it during the player's combat turn when a click does nothing (an empty floor tile out of reach, or any tile out of the aimed artifact's range or that it can't target), and raises `PlayerTurn.ClickRefused`.
- `TileSearch` (`Map/Tiles/Search`: `CircleTileSearch`, a BFS, and `LineTileSearch`) computes ranges, distances and paths, each tile found kept as a `TileWrapper` (the tile, the one it was reached from, its distance). The `TileConstraints` delegates (walkable, empty, line of sight, no collectable) chain on it: `Selecting(...)` filters the tiles found, `Through(...)` the tiles the search goes through. A `CircleTileSearch` keeps its visited set and queue between searches. `TileSearch.Movement` is the walking search: it never paths through an entity or a collectable, which can only be the destination. `TileSearchConfig` describes one in the data assets.
- `CombatGrid` (`Visuals`, on `Managers/Gameplay.prefab`) draws a thin grid over the walkable tiles during the deploy phase and the combat, faded in on `DeployStarted` / `CombatStarted` and out on `CombatEnded`, hidden at once when disabled (the Classic [edition](editions.md)). It builds one mesh per room on its first entry, a flat quad per tile on its top; `Rendering/CombatGrid.shader` (`Mat_CombatGrid`) draws the borders at a constant width in pixels, half by each tile for a border shared with a neighbour at the same height. Render queues: the grid at Transparent-1 (after the rift's air), the selection overlays at Transparent, the entity rings (see [Entities](entities.md#components)) at Transparent+1.
- `PathLine` (`Visuals`, on `Managers/Gameplay.prefab`) draws the path the player would walk to the hovered tile in combat (`PlayerMove.PathPreviewed`): a world space `LineRenderer` lying flat over the tiles' tops (`height` above them), from `startGap` out of the player's tile to the edge of a ring on the hovered tile (`Path End`, a flat quad pulsing by `endPulse` at `endPulseSpeed`, `Mat_PathEnd.mat` with `PathEnd.png`). The line is texture mode Tile with `Art/Materials/Tiles/Mat_PathLine.mat` (`PathChevron.png`: a soft band with a bright core and one chevron, twice as long as wide, repeated `chevronsPerMeter` times), the chevrons flowing towards the hovered tile at `flowSpeed` (`_BaseMap_ST` offset in a property block). Disabled (the Classic [edition](editions.md)), it hides and ignores the previews. Both materials are URP particles, additive, in an HDR lilac of the energy color that the bloom catches, queue Transparent+2.
- `Room.Tiles` (exits included) and `Room.TileBounds` (the box around the tiles' centers) are gathered once in its `Awake`: the systems reacting to `RoomEntered` read them instead of scanning the room.
- `Room` raises the static `TileHovered` (every change of the tile under the pointer; none over a UI element or while a menu is open), `EntityHovered` (every change of the entity on that tile, and when the same entity goes from being pointed at from the UI to being hovered on the board or back) `TileClicked` (on a selected tile) and `UnselectableTileClicked` (on any other tile) events; `Room.HoveredTile` and `HoveredEntity` are the tile under the pointer and its entity. `Tile.FindHoveredTile` raycasts each frame against the tiles and the entities (Terrain, Player and Enemy layers, triggers included): the nearest tile hit wins, even through an entity's model, so that the tiles behind a tall model stay easy to pick; an entity's collider or `Hover` child (a trigger box over its model on the `Player`, `Enemy` and `Golem` prefabs; the raycasts of the game ignore triggers, so it doesn't block the lines of sight) stands for its tile only when no tile is behind it (the edge of the board), and a dying entity lets the pointer through. In the Classic [edition](editions.md) (`modelPicking` off), it raycasts the Terrain layer only, as the original; with `infoOnModelHover` (on in the Classic), `Room` takes the hovered entity from `Tile.FindHoveredModel` first (the living entity whose model or `Hover` box is nearer than the terrain), so that the enemy info shows on the model as the original's, whatever tile is picked behind it. `Room.PointAt(entity)` / `StopPointingAt` make the board act as if the pointer were on an entity's tile (the HUD's timeline); `Room.IsPointedFromUI` tells it, and the board's enemy info panel then stays hidden (the timeline's tooltip shows the info). `Room` clears the old tile's pointer highlight before raising `TileHovered` and sets the new one's after the listeners, so that the modes' `Tile.ResetTargetTiles` never blanks it for a frame.

## Water

The water is scenery: nothing enters it. A pool is `Prefabs/Environment/Water/Water.prefab` (or its sibling `Water_Drareg`, for the Antechamber and the boss room): Unity's cube scaled to the pool (the top face is the surface), on the `Water` layer, with a `WaterSurface` (`Visuals`) and `Art/Materials/Environment/Mat_Water.mat` or `Mat_Water_Drareg.mat`. Move and scale the cube to fit the hollow it fills. Its box collider is a trigger: the `WaterDrip` raycasts find it, and the snowflakes vanish in it. `WaterSurface` also passes the enabled pools' boxes to the shaders (`_WaterPools`, 8 at most): Snow Lit wets the rock in a band just over their waterline (`WaterWetness` in `Water.hlsl`: no snow, darker and glossy). The snowfall doesn't collide with the `Water` layer; `WaterSplash`, on the `Snow` and `SnowNear` systems of `Environment/Snow`, lists the enabled pools' colliders in their Trigger module (again whenever `WaterSurface.Version` changes), kills the flakes entering one and leaves a small ring where each touched (`WaterRipples.Splash`, the last 48).

`Rendering/Water.shader` ("Towards the Unknown/Water", queue `Transparent-60`, before the rift's air; it writes the depth and composes the whole color from the opaque texture) follows the view through the box to the ground under it (the depth texture, rebuilt in world space: the camera is orthographic) or out of its bottom and down `_FloorDepth` more:

- The water absorbs along that path, red first (`_Absorption` per meter and channel), and turns to `_DeepColor`, brightened by the lights (`_Turbidity`) and its own glow (`_ScatterEmission`, the green shimmer of Drareg's water).
- The ground is refracted by the ripples (`_Refraction`); a ground above the surface is not, so the rocks around don't bleed in. It blurs as it lies deeper (`_DepthBlur`, four taps kept only under the water). Caustics (`_Caustics`, two drifting cell patterns) light it where the main or an additional light reaches it, with its shadows and cookie, fading with depth.
- A sharp line (`_EdgeColor`, `_EdgeWidth`, a second thinner line, cut by a noise) outlines everything crossing the surface, where the ground rises within `_EdgeWidth` of it.
- The surface breathes: its top vertices follow `WaterWave` of `Rendering/Water.hlsl` (`_WaveSpeed`, `_WaveFrequency`, `_WaveScale`; a frequency of 0 raises the whole surface together), the function the floating plants use (see [vegetation](#vegetation)): a lily pad whose material has the same three values as the pool rises with it.
- The ripples are in the normals only: `Art/Textures/Environment/WaterNormal.jpg` in two layers drifting apart, a third running with the wind and the lot roughened where a gust of `Wind` passes (`WindGustAt`, `_GustRipple`), rings scattered by a passing gust (`_GustRings`), the rings of the snowflakes touching it (small, their crest catching a little light: `_SplashGlow`) and of the drops (`WaterRipples.Add`, sent by a `WaterDrip` under a stalactite, `Prefabs/Environment/Water/WaterDrip.prefab`: it drops its particle now and then, living just as long as its fall, rings where it lands on the pool below and posts its Wwise event; a few are placed under the stalactites hanging over the pools).
- A current (`_Flow`, xz in meters per second) carries the ripples, the line's noise and the caustics: `Mat_Water_Stream`, on the channel of CombatRoom10 running out of the view.
- It mirrors the room (`_ReflectionStrength`, blurred with the ripples through the mips) and catches the lights (`_Specular`, clamped by `_SpecularClamp`), with glints flashing on the ripples where a light strikes them (`_Glints`).
- The sides show the water in cross section, with a line at the waterline.

`WaterReflection`, on the game camera (`Managers/Gameplay`, Main Camera) and on the menu's, draws the planar reflection: each frame a `WaterSurface` is visible, a camera placed as the mirror image of the view under the plane of the largest visible pool renders its `cullingMask` (not `Water`, `UI`, nor `Not in Reflection`, where the RiftVolume and the snowfall are) at a third of the resolution, without shadows or post-processing, into `_WaterReflectionTex`. It is a proper camera looking up from below, so its image is flipped left to right (the shader reads `1 - u`) and the faces keep their winding; an oblique near plane clips what lies under the water. A pool at another height than the plane reads the same image shifted on screen by twice the difference, exact in orthographic projection. In the editor at 4K it costs under half a millisecond of GPU; the shadows add about 0.2 ms.

## Ambience

`Prefabs/Environment/Snow.prefab`, placed once in the game and test scenes over the rooms (which are all loaded at the same place), holds the weather shared by every room:

- The snowfall: fine flakes falling straight down with a light wind, over a 34 m square. They collide with the colliders (high quality collisions: tiles, entities), stop where they land, puff (the SnowImpact sub emitter) and melt away. A few big soft flakes (SnowNear) drift slowly and give the snowfall its depth. The Play On Awake setting is shared by all the systems of the effect: keep it on.
- Wisps of mist drifting in the void 1.5 to 4 m below the tiles (the Mist object): a few dozen large, flat, soft particles moving along the wind and wandering (Noise module), pushed by the gusts' WindZone (External Forces). Their shape comes from `Rendering/Mist.shader` ("Towards the Unknown/Mist", `Art/Materials/Environment/Particle_MistWisp.mat`), not from a texture: a warped noise that keeps churning, so that a wisp changes shape as it drifts; the particle system passes its UV, Stable Random X and Age Percent as TEXCOORD0 (Custom Vertex Streams: keep them). It is blended over the scene, lit where a light of the room crosses it, faded where it meets the rock (a depth fade that handles the orthographic camera) and above `_FadeTop`, and drawn at Transparent-55: after the water (Transparent-60), so that it drifts over the pools, and before the RiftVolume, which darkens it with the depth.
- Fine dust (Dust, a lit additive particle material) that only shows where light crosses it.
In the Classic [edition](editions.md), the root's `EditionOnly` stops all of this (the systems but the flakes' renderer, `SnowCover`, `RiftLighting`, `Wind`, `WaterSplash`) and the other shows `SnowClassic`, a nested instance of the original snowfall (`Art/Classic/Prefabs/Environment/Snow_Classic.prefab`, restored from main, at its 2022 place and angle).

- `RiftLighting`: the rooms are at the bottom of a rift. On each room entry, the room's main light (its brightest spot) gets the rift cookie (`Art/Textures/Environment/RiftCookie.png`: a main opening, thin cracks and holes, almost no light elsewhere but a faint bounce around the board), a stronger intensity, and a lean and turn of its own (seeded by the room's name), once; it keeps each light's cookie, intensity and rotation, and gives them back when disabled (the Classic edition), then lights the current room again when enabled. It also fits the RiftVolume box to the room: `Rendering/RiftVolumetrics.shader` ray marches through it up to the first visible surface, gathering the additional lights (with their cookies and shadows) scattered by drifting dust, which shows the rays, and a dark mist thickening below the tiles, in banks that drift with the volume's `_Wind` (`_MistDrift`) and slowly change shape (`_MistChurn`): a noise stretched horizontally (`_MistBankScale`) and warped, `_MistBanks` going from an even layer to banks with clear gaps. `_ShaftKnee` keeps dim light from lighting the air, so that only the rays stand out. The air and the mist also absorb (`_Extinction` per meter, `_MistExtinction`): the volume is blended over the scene by its transmittance, so the back of the cave and the void darken instead of turning milky, while the glowing crystals deep in the rift still show faintly.
- `Wind`: the wind of the vegetation (see [vegetation](#vegetation)), blowing the way the snowfall drifts, and now and then a gust.
- `SnowCover`: on each `GameEvents.RoomEntered`, draws the height of the room's Snow Lit meshes seen from the top (`Rendering/SnowHeight.shader`, highest one kept) into a global half float map (`_SnowHeightMap`, `_SnowHeightBounds`). The hanging meshes listed in its `hangingMeshes` (the LowPolyCavePack stalactites, Rock_D, and chains, Chain_A) are left out, so that snow settles under them. It also passes the room's heat sources (`SnowHeat`, on the flame lights of `FireTorch` and `FireCandle`, with their radius; 16 at most) to `_SnowHeatSources`: Snow Lit melts the snow around each one, in a patch whose edge a noise pushes in and out, near the source's height only, and darkens the cleared ground as if damp. The height map and the heat sources are read through `Rendering/Snow.hlsl` (`SkyExposure`, `HeatMelt`), shared by Snow Lit and the grass.

The rocks, props, cave (`Art/Materials/Environment/Mat_Snow*`, taken out of the LowPolyCavePack once reworked) and tiles use `Rendering/SnowLit.shader` ("Towards the Unknown/Snow Lit"): a lit surface where snow settles on the faces turned upwards (`_SnowAmount` for the slope, `_SnowCoverage` for the share of those faces, in noisy patches), stays off the surfaces sheltered by something above them (the height map), and glints (sparkles, rim, a cold blue on its unlit side). The tiles keep a light, patchy dusting so that the board stays readable. With its `_WIND` keyword on (`Mat_SnowPlants`, `Mat_SnowPlants_Cave`, in `Art/Materials/Nature`), Snow Lit moves in the wind like the plants, the snow staying where it lay on the mesh: the snowy trees and branches use it. Never turn the wind on in `Mat_SnowProps`, shared by the rocks and props; the meadow roots (`Root_B_01` to `_06` but `_03`) use its copy `Mat_SnowRoots`, the original having another material there (see [editions](editions.md)). The particle materials are in `Art/Materials/Environment`, their generated textures in `Art/Textures/Environment`. The LowPolyCavePack's FX materials use legacy shaders that URP doesn't render: build URP materials from their textures instead. In the editor, a shader just edited shows cyan while its variants compile: wait before judging a capture.

The crystals (`Prefabs/Environment/Crystals`, meshes in `Art/Models/Crystals`, taken out of the LowPolyCavePack) use `Mat_MagicCrystal` and `Rendering/MagicCrystal.shader` ("Towards the Unknown/Magic Crystal"): a dark glossy shell lit by the scene over an HDR inner glow, deep blue at the base and brighter blue violet at the tips, with veins of light seen at a depth (a parallax in object space), a violet rim, facets that shimmer in turn and a slow pulse seeded by each crystal's position.

The flames of `FireTorch` and `FireCandle` flicker through their light's `LightFlicker` (not in the Classic [edition](editions.md), which also turns off their `SnowHeat`); their flames (`Prefabs/VFX/TorchFlame` and `CandleFlame`, textures in `Art/VFX/Flame`) are prewarmed, so they burn fully as soon as a room loads. In the braziers (`ZLPC_Torch_06`), the flame sits centered in the bowl and its light just above it, kept low (intensity 1.1, range 6) so that the bowl doesn't burn out. The lanterns of the combat rooms are `Prefabs/Environment/Lantern` (the gallows post, LowPolyCavePack's `ZLPC_Lamp_01` mesh) and its variants `LanternCurved` (`ZLPC_Lamp_02`) and `LanternArm` (`ZLPC_Lamp_03`, the arm alone): one flickering point light at the middle of the glass (intensity 4, range 6.5, the range not following the instance's scale), in place of the pack's three lights under the lantern. The lantern's mesh is on the `Lantern` layer, left out of the light's culling mask (the renderer is Forward, which honors it): lit from a few centimeters, it would burn out white. Place those, not the pack's `ZLPC_Lamp_*` prefabs: the rooms keep the pack's lamps of the original release only for the Classic [edition](editions.md), shown by an `EditionOnly` of their root while the lanterns, the grass, the drips and the plants the original didn't have are hidden by another. The antechamber holds its own global volume (`Prefabs/Volume/AntechamberVolume`, priority 1): a warmer, more contrasted grade over the game's, active while the room is.

In the boss room (`BossRoom1`), floating pieces of decor bob up and down with `FloatObject` (a sine of `floatStrength` meters at `floatSpeed` around its start position, in game time).

## Vegetation

The plants are in `Nature` folders, by family (`Mushrooms`, `Vines`, `Trees`, `Roots`, `Plants`, `WaterPlants`, `Grass`): models in `Art/Models/Nature`, materials in `Art/Materials/Nature`, textures in `Art/Textures/Nature`, prefabs in `Prefabs/Environment/Nature`. The cave pack's mushrooms, vines, tree and roots, the meadows pack's trees, branches and roots and Glafira's plants (the tree of life and the dead tree, the wisteria and its strands, the lily pads, the flowering plants) were taken out of their packs there and renamed; place those prefabs. The rooms place them as prefab instances.

Everything that grows moves in the wind, but the trunks barely and the roots not at all. The plants use `Rendering/NatureLit.shader` ("Towards the Unknown/Nature Lit"), whose looks are keywords:

| Keyword | Look | Materials |
|---|---|---|
| (none) | A lit surface, a base map and color, metallic and smoothness from `_MaskMap` | the vines, the cave tree, the petals and stems of Glafira's plants |
| `_EMISSION` | An emission map times an HDR color | `Mat_Nature_Mushroom` |

### Wind

`Rendering/Wind.hlsl` moves a vertex in world space, in meters, so that a plant sways the same at any scale. Each material sets how far the top leans (`_WindBend`), how far the tips shiver (`_WindFlutter`), how far the entities push it (`_WindPush`) and a mask (`_WindMask`), which part moves:

- Height: bends from the pivot, nothing at its foot, the most at `_WindHeight` above it (the vines, the trees, the plants).
- Hanging: swings from the top (the wisteria's strands).
- Rigid: tilts as a whole around its pivot (the mushrooms).
- Floating: rides the water's wave at its pivot, tilted by its slope, and drifts a little (the lily pads, whose `_WaveSpeed`, `_WaveFrequency` and `_WaveScale` must match the pool's material).

The lean follows the breeze, a slow sway around it and the gusts; each plant takes its phase from its pivot. The wind is applied in the four passes (forward, shadows, depth, depth normals), so the shadows and the ambient occlusion follow.

`Wind` (on `Environment/Snow`) sets the global values each frame:

- The breeze: its heading (the way the snowfall drifts), its strength (0 is still) and the speeds of the sway and the flutter.
- The gusts: now and then (a random interval), a front crosses the room along the wind at `gustSpeed`, each gust a little stronger or weaker, bending the plants as it passes. It carries `Prefabs/Environment/WindGust`, instantiated as a child and deactivated with `Wind` (the Classic [edition](editions.md)): a few long trails of wind (the particles only draw their trails; they stop and fade against the decor) and a little snow lifted from the ground. Its WindZone pushes the snowflakes (External Forces on the Snow and SnowNear systems, each flake taking a random share of it, so that some are carried away and the others keep falling).
- The entities of the turn system (16 at most): the grass and the small plants bend away from their feet (`_WindPush`).
- The hit waves: a hit at least `waveMinWeight` heavy (the weight `ImpactFeedback` gives it, raised as `ImpactFeedback.HitWeighed`: the same one as the shake and the hit stop, heavier on the player), or a death, sends a ring from the entity that lays the plants down as it passes, stronger with the weight (4 at most at once, in a `ShaderRingBuffer`, the helper the water's rings use too).

The shaders' clock is the scaled time: the wind and the waves stop during a hit stop.

A plant made of several meshes (Glafira's trees and flowers, the packs' trees) has a `WindAnchor` on its root: it passes to its parts the point they bend from and the plant's height, through a property block (those renderers leave the SRP Batcher; disabled, it clears the block, the materials' own anchor being zero), so that they move as one. With `hangFromTop`, each part swings from the top of its bounds instead (the wisteria).

### Grass

The grass tufts and flower clumps are generated meshes (`Art/Models/Nature/Grass`, Tools > Nature > Generate Grass, `GrassMeshGenerator`): low poly blades seen from both sides, whose vertex color holds the height along the blade (the wind's weight and the color's gradient), a tint per blade and the petals, which glow. The grass mode of Nature Lit colors them from the foot to the tip, darkens their foot, and puts snow on the tips where the sky is open (the height map of `SnowCover`), melted around the flames.

A `GrassPatch` (`Prefabs/Environment/Nature/Grass`: `GrassPatch`, `GrassPatch_Snowy`, `GrassPatch_Flowers`) holds one line per tuft (place, turn, size, mesh) and draws them instanced, one draw per mesh, casting shadows. Its Scatter button drops the tufts under the patch on the ground only: the materials listed in `grounds` (the rocks, the cliffs, the cave), flat and wide enough, never on a tile, a structure, a small stone or another plant. Scatter needs colliders on the decor, and most decor has none: add temporary Mesh Colliders, scatter, then remove them. The rooms hold their patches as plain objects (unpacked), in the decor around the board, where the camera sees them.
