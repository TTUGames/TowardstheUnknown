# Inventory

Artifacts are placed in Tetris-style grids (`Inventory/TetrisInventory*`), 5x5 by default.

| Class | Role |
|---|---|
| `TetrisInventoryData` | A grid's model: its items, their slots and rotations, and the list of their artifacts in the order they were added (an item moved within its grid, or sent back home, keeps its place: the skills keep their keys); raises `Changed` |
| `TetrisInventoryItem` | An artifact in a grid, with its slot and rotation (0, 90, 180, 270) |
| `TetrisInventory` | The view: shows a grid (`Show`), draws it in a UI Toolkit element once bound (`Bind`) and follows its changes (`Sync`: the slots are rebuilt only when the grid's size changes, and only the pieces of the items removed and added are, the others keeping their element and their animations' phase; a change during a chest's reveal shows the pieces still hidden at once, `ArtifactPiece.Unconceal`); previews placements and highlights the hovered item |
| `InventoryDrag` | Moves the artifacts between the open grids with the pointer events; the `Rotate` action turns the held artifact. A double click on an artifact (`TryStore`) puts it in the first slot of the other open grid it fits in, from the chest to the player's grid or back, trying its four rotations, or leaves it refused. Closing the inventory keeps the chest shown while the screen fades out: the character sheet comes back on the next opening. A press becomes a drag past `EditionProfile.inventoryDragThreshold` points (6, the Classic 10); the piece is held where it was grabbed, or by its first slot's center in the Classic (`grabWhereClicked` off). A piece put down plays `artifactDrop`, or `artifactRefused` where it doesn't fit (`refusalFeedback`) |
| `ArtifactPiece` | The element drawing an item: its piece, generated from the artifact (see [Pieces](#pieces)) |

## Pieces

An item is not a drawn sprite: `TetrisInventory.CreateItemImage` builds an `ArtifactPiece` (`Scripts/UI/Components`) from the artifact's shape, rarity and skill bar icon, 80 points per cell (`TetrisInventory.CellSize`). The geometry lives in `ArtifactPieceLayout`, shared with the inspector preview so that both always agree:

- The outline is inset by a 2-point `Gap`, so that two pieces side by side keep a gap, and drawn with `Painter2D` as one shape in the rarity's `Surface` tone, with a line running inside it, in the `--piece-line-color` of `Inventory.uss` (the accent's pale tint while hovered).
- The icon (`skillBarIcon`, the same sprite as the skills bar) is laid on the shape's largest rectangle (`LargestRectangle`: the largest, then the squarest, then the most centered), never stretched: whole (`ArtifactIconFit.Contain`) or covering it (`Fill`), then scaled, turned by quarter turns and offset in cells as `ArtifactData` says (`inventoryIconFit`, `inventoryIconScale`, `inventoryIconRotation`, `inventoryIconOffset`). Each cell is an `overflow: hidden` element holding its part of the icon, so that the shape cuts it; cells overlap their neighbors by a point so that no seam shows.
- The placement frames the drawing, not the sprite: `inventoryIconBounds` is where the opaque pixels lie in the sprite. It is read-only in the inspector and set by **Tools > Artifacts > Measure Icons** (`ArtifactIconTools`, reading the source PNG since the imported texture is not readable): run it after changing or adding an icon, or the icon keeps the old framing.
- A UI Toolkit filter animates the piece by its rarity (`UI/Filters/ArtifactPiece.shader`, `.mat` and `.asset`, referenced by `GameAssets.artifactPieceEffect`): a drifting nebula, veins of the glow from rare on, a sheen, gold and sparkles for legendary, the icon pulsing. `ArtifactPiece` sets a new filter about 30 times a second with the rarity's `Glow` tone, the rarity plus a random seed (so that the pieces don't sweep together), the aspect and the time, while it is shown (`ElementVisibility.IsShown`: not while the inventory is closed; the time picks the animation up where it would have been). The filter tells the icon from the surface by its near-white pixels: the icons must stay white.
- Motion: taken in the hand the piece shrinks a little around the point grabbed (`Hold`, class `artifact-piece--held`); put down it grows back past its size and settles (`Land`, played by `TetrisInventory` on the item just added, `ease-out-back`); dropped where it doesn't fit or outside the grids, it goes back home and shakes, its outline in the accent color (`LandRefused`, `TetrisInventory.AddItem(..., refused: true)`, `artifact-piece--refused`, `RefuseShake`); turned it swings a quarter turn around the same point, which stays under the pointer (`PlayTurn`, called by `InventoryDrag`). In the Classic [edition](editions.md), the refused shake and the swing are left out (`refusalFeedback`, `pieceTurnAnimation`), the original having neither. The grid places and turns the piece around its bottom left corner, so the shrink and the shake are on an inner `artifact-piece__body` and the swing on an `artifact-piece__spin` inside it; don't animate the piece element itself. The styles are in `Styles/Inventory.uss`; the slots are drawn in USS (a thin square outline, `.inventory-slot`), without a texture (the Classic's wear the original's `Case_test.png`, `ClassicInventory.uss`).

In the `ArtifactData` inspector, `[PiecePreview]` on `inventoryIconBounds` draws the piece as the inventory shows it, without the animation (`PiecePreviewAttributeDrawer`), to tune the icon by eye. The artifact icons are in `Art/UI/Artifacts`, named after their artifact and packed by the `SkillIcons` atlas.

### Rarity palette

`RarityPalette` (`Assets/Data/RarityPalette.asset`) holds the rarities' design tokens: one hue per rarity, indexed by `ArtifactRarity` (common, rare, epic, legendary), and a few tones (`Surface` for the pieces, `Accent` for lines and highlights (the reveal's flash, the rarity's name in the artifact's info), `Glow` for the HDR light of the relics), each a brightness and a mix towards white. Every use asks `Get(rarity, tone)`, so that changing a hue changes the pieces and the relics together; a use may scale what it gets (`RelicAura.glow`), never pick its own color. The inventory reaches it through `InventoryScreen.rarityPalette` (on the `UI` prefab), which `TetrisInventory.Bind` passes to the `ArtifactPiece`s it creates; the relics through their `palette` field.

## Player inventory

`InventoryManager` on `Player.prefab` owns the player's grid (`Data`), filled with its `startingArtifacts` on first use, and raises `ArtifactsChanged`. `TetrisInventoryData.Replace` empties a grid and fills it with other artifacts, those that don't fit left out (the combat sandbox's sets). The skills bar lists its artifacts, in order.

## Screen

`InventoryScreen` (`GameScene.UI.Inventory`, opened by `ToggleInventory` or the HUD's bag button) shows the player's grid, with its slots taken under it (`GridCount`, UI key `InventoryGridCount`: "13 / 25 cases", the piece in hand counted brighter, `grid-count--preview`, from `InventoryDrag.HandChanged`, in the accent once the grid is full, `grid-count--full`; hidden in the Classic), and next to it the character sheet (stats, the run's progress: the kills of each of its `killFamilies` (`EntityData`, in the label `<name>Count`, UI key `PlayerProgress<name>Count`) and the rooms visited, and the zone text: Drareg's garden in the antechamber and the boss room, the absolute zero elsewhere) or a chest's grid, plus the info of the last hovered or pressed artifact, the first artifact's until one is (the last pressed one only in the Classic [edition](editions.md), `hoverArtifactInfo` off, and nothing until one is pressed, `prefillArtifactInfo` off). The bottom of the artifact's panel recalls the controls (UI key `InventoryControlsHint`: drag, double click, R or right click, Escape; hidden in the Classic). The info writes the artifact's rarity at the right of its title (`ArtifactRarity`, UI keys `RarityCommon` to `RarityLegendary`) in its rarity's `Accent` tone, brightened to at least 0.8 so that the common's dark grey reads (hidden in the Classic, whose pieces carry their colors). The cooldown's disc on the icon's frame is hidden for an artifact without cooldown (`artifact-info__cooldown--none`: its text gives its uses per turn), shown with its 0 in the Classic as the original's. A piece also holds its artifact's original sprite (`classicInventorySprite`), hidden unless the Classic's style shows it.

## Chests

Treasures draw from `ArtifactPool` assets (`Assets/Data/ArtifactPools`): a list of `ArtifactPoolElement`s, each a group of artifacts with a `weight`; `GetRandomElement` picks one group by weight and gives all its artifacts, and an empty group spawns nothing. Each `TreasureSpawnPoint` references its pool:

| Pool | Used by | Groups |
|---|---|---|
| `TreasureRoomArtifactPool` | `TreasureRoom01` | One artifact each, weighted by rarity: 45 rare, 30 epic, 15 legendary; no common |
| `CombatRoomArtifactPool` | `CombatRoom.prefab`, the reward of every combat room | 22 groups of one common or rare artifact (and `ExplosiveSacrifice`, epic; `Strike` twice) at weight 1, and an empty group at 20: about half the combats (20 / 42) give nothing |
| `SpawnRoomArtifactPool` | `SpawnRoom01` | One rare artifact each, weight 1 |
| `AntechamberArtifactPool` | `Antechamber` | One legendary artifact each, weight 1 |

The treasure rooms, the spawn room and the antechamber spawn theirs as they load, through their `TreasureSpawnLayout` (see [generation](map.md#generation)); a combat room spawns its reward on `CombatEnded` (`Room.SpawnReward`, at its first `TreasureSpawnPoint`), after the victory's beat, growing out of its tile (`Collectable.PopIn`; at once in the Classic [edition](editions.md), `victoryBeat` and `rewardPopIn`). `TreasureSpawnPoint` spawns a `Collectable` showing an aura of its best rarity: a variant of `Prefabs/VFX/Drop/RelicDrop` (`CommonDrop`, `RareDrop`, `EpicDrop`, `LegendaryDrop`), an unstable orb floating over the tile (in the Classic [edition](editions.md), the original's aura instead: `Collectable` instantiates the prefab `ClassicSkin` pairs with it, again when the edition changes). `RelicAura` gives the rarity's glow tone from the [rarity palette](#rarity-palette), its glow scale and glitch clock to the orb (`Rendering/RelicOrb.shader`: a dark glass shell over a swirling nebula, rifts of light, an echo of itself from another world, slices slipping sideways when it glitches) and to the space around it (`Rendering/RelicDistortion.shader`: reads the opaque texture behind the orb, bends it, drains a ring of void around it and, in step with the orb, slips its slices and splits its colors; the orb's own pixels, found by their depth, are spared), plus the fragments breaking off (`RelicMote.shader`) and the hue to a small light. `Rendering/Relic.hlsl` holds their shared seed and glitch clock, and includes the noise (`Rendering/Noise.hlsl`). The variants set their `rarity` and override the glow scale, glitch chance, fragment rate and light intensity; the color is never theirs to pick (the fragment and light colors serialized in the variants are only placeholders: `RelicAura` sets them in play, on enable; in the editor it only previews the orb and the distortion, through property blocks, so that a palette change never rewrites the prefabs). It registers on the tile under it (`Tile.Collectable`) so that movement paths go around it: it is only reached by clicking its tile. The pointer on that tile, or on the orb itself (`Collectable.FindPointed`, within its `pickRadius`: the orb picks its tile as an entity's model does, `modelPicking`), hovers the relic: `Collectable` follows `Room.TileHovered` and tells its aura's `RelicHover`, which eases `_Hover` in and out and gives `_HoverPulse` (the seconds since the pointer came) to the orb and the distortion (property blocks, next to `RelicAura`'s). The relic steadies: its glitches and tremor die down with the distortion's, its echo fades away, and it lights up without growing (a slight swell and lift): its rifts open a little and glow, a faint band of light sweeps up it, after a short flash and a thin shockwave running out as the pointer arrives. The hover's light is in the rarity's hue, never dimmer than `_HoverFloor` (the brightness shown, the aura's glow included), so that it reads on the dark common orb without blowing out. Its light brightens (`lightBoost`, a `lightFlash`) and lights the floor under it, the fragments burst (`moteBurst`) and thicken (`moteBoost`), and `hoverSound` plays; the hovered tile takes the board's usual highlight. The Classic's aura has no `RelicHover`: its relic stays still under the pointer, as the original's. Walking into it opens the inventory with a chest grid of its artifacts (`InventoryScreen.OpenChest`), to drag from; in the Anniversary [edition](editions.md) (`chestReveal`), the relic first bursts in its best rarity's color, with that rarity's sound (`Collectable.openSounds`, posted on the player), and the chest opens 0.1 s later, its pieces coming in one by one, the rarest last, each flashing in its rarity's accent (`TetrisInventory.Reveal`, `ArtifactPiece.Reveal`: `artifact-piece--concealed`, then `--revealing`); the artifacts left in it are lost once closed: with some left, a first closing (I, Tab, Escape or the bag) shakes the chest's grid, plays the refusal sound and shows a warning under it (`ChestWarning`, UI key `ChestLossWarning`) for its `--confirm-duration` (3 s), and only a second closing within it closes (`InventoryScreen.AskBeforeLosingChest`; at once in the Classic, `confirmations` off). A collectable left in a room stays in it: the room is kept, deactivated, until the next visit. Its room counts it as its loot (`Room.CountLoot`): the minimap and the exits leading there show it in gold until it is picked up (see [exits](map.md#exits)).
