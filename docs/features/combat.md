# Combat

## Abilities

Artifacts (the player's skills) and enemy patterns share `AbilityData` (`Combat/Abilities`), a ScriptableObject edited with Odin:

| Group | Fields |
|---|---|
| Targeting | `target` (`EntityType` hit), `range` and, if `isAreaOfEffect`, `area` (`TileSearchConfig`: a shape and a min/max distance) |
| Effects | `castEffects`, applied once with the caster as target; `effects`, applied to each target |
| Animation | `animationClip` (played by the caster's `EntityAnimator`, none if empty), `followUpClip` (its second part, e.g. the Golem's shock wave jump then landing), `animationSpeed`, `duration` (time the next actions wait: at most 1.2 s for a common artifact, 2 s for a rare or epic one, 3 s for a legendary one, 1.5 s for an enemy, Drareg's ultimate aside), `vfxDuration` (how long the VFX play from the start of the attack, even once the next actions go on), `impactDelay` (from the start of the animation to the strike: the moment the effects apply, or the projectile leaves; 0.5 s by default), `timing` (`AttackTiming`, how the clip plays in time, see [attack timing](#attack-timing)), `legs` (how much a humanoid's legs follow the clip, its upper body always does: 0 keeps the stance's, see [animation](entities.md#animation)), `projectile` (`ProjectileInfo`, none if its prefab is empty), `vfx` (`VFXInfo` list), `sound` (Wwise event) |

`Ability` is the runtime base, holding the range and area searches: `CanTarget(tile)`, `CanReach(from, tile)`, `GetTargets(tile)` and `Cast(caster, tile, chained, chainedRecovery)`; `IsAreaOfEffect` tells an area from a single target. `Cast` picks the targets, turns the caster towards the tile, posts the sound and queues, in order: an `AttackAnimationAction` (animation and VFX, done at the impact: after `impactDelay` as the [attack timing](#attack-timing) plays it, then the flight of the projectile if any), the effects of `castEffects` then of `effects` on each target, and an `AttackRecoveryAction` that waits for the rest of `duration` after the impact (`AttackAnimationAction.ImpactTime`), then ends the attack (`AttackAnimationAction.End`: lets a move cut the rest of the caster's clip, `EntityAnimator.EndAttack`, and calls `PlayerAttack.EndAttackVisuals`, which takes the player's neon color off and dissolves the weapons, after any attack, an enemy's included). The VFX outlive the attack: `ReleaseVFXLater` removes them `vfxDuration` after its start (at once if that time has passed), so that the duration only covers the strike and a short recovery and never waits for an effect to finish. When the optional `chained` returns true (a [queued cast](entities.md#cast-queue) follows), the recovery ends after `chainedRecovery` seconds so that the next cast starts, and the VFX of the cut attack play out the same way. The hits, damage numbers and deaths thus land at the impact, not when the attack starts, in both editions (the original applied them at the cast). No animation carries a hit event: tune `impactDelay` on the asset to match its animation and VFX (the strike of a clip is where its weapon or hands move fastest: the `attack-inspect` skill measures it).

### Artifacts

`ArtifactData` (`Assets/Data/Artifacts`) adds the skill bar icon (also drawn on its inventory piece), rarity, cost, uses per turn (0 for unlimited), cooldown, the player's neon color and weapon while casting, and the inventory shape with the placement of the icon on the piece ([inventory pieces](inventory.md#pieces)). Its asset name is its ID, key of its localized texts. `Artifact` is its runtime instance (`data.CreateArtifact()`), holding the cooldown and remaining uses. The cooldown is counted down at the start of the player's turns, before the artifact can be cast: a cooldown of N blocks it for N - 1 turns after the one it was cast in (1: once per turn), which is what `CooldownDescription` shows (`cooldown - 1`, or the uses per turn without a cooldown). `Pay` takes the energy, a use and, on the last use (every use with unlimited uses), starts the cooldown; `Refund` gives them back (the cooldown only if that payment started it) for a [queued cast](entities.md#cast-queue) dropped before it plays. With `PlayerStats.Unlimited` (the combat sandbox), `CanUse` always holds and `Pay` and `Refund` take and give nothing. `PlayerAttack` pays, then calls `Cast`, at once or later.

### Enemy patterns

`EnemyPatternData` (`Assets/Data/EnemyPatterns`, named `<Enemy><Attack>Pattern`) adds nothing to `AbilityData` but a `Reset` that sets its `target` to `PLAYER`: only the patterns targeting the player are cast (see [enemies](entities.md#enemies)). `EnemyPattern` is its runtime instance; `CanTarget(currentTile, target)` checks the target type and range. Enemies list their patterns in an `EnemyPatternSet` (see [Entities](entities.md#enemies)).

### Attack timing

An attack's clip does not play at a constant speed: `AbilityData.timing` (`AttackTiming`, `enabled`) shapes it into an anticipation (`windupSpeed`) slowing into a pose held at `swingStart` (`windupHold`, real seconds), a fast swing (`swingSpeed`) to the strike (`strike`, the impact if 0: a spell's gesture ends before its effect lands), a pose held on it (`strikeHold`), then a recovery easing back to `recoverySpeed`; `recovery` (real seconds, 0 for the rest of `duration`) is how long the next actions wait after the impact while the phases play, which shortens the attack in the Anniversary only (`AttackAnimationAction.Recovery`, read by `AttackRecoveryAction`). Its positions are in seconds of the clip at the ability's speed, like `impactDelay` and the VFX delays, which were set on a clip played at a constant speed. `AbilityData.Clock()` plays it out for the clip the edition shows into an `AttackClock`: the clip's position at each real second (`Position`, a table computed at 240 Hz; a held pose creeps at 6 % of the speed rather than freezing) and back (`TimeAt`). `EventTime` places an event (the impact, a VFX's delay) with the pose up to the strike and, after it, as long after the strike as it was set: the flight of a spell keeps its delay whatever the held pose. `AttackAnimationAction` builds the clock at its start (`Clock`), waits for `EventTime(impactDelay)`, and `VFXInfo` for `EventTime(delay)`; `EntityAnimator.PlayAttack` sets the clip's time from it at each frame. Off, or in an edition whose `attackTiming` is off (the Classic), the clock is linear: the clip, the impact and the VFX play as set. An ability without a clip (Rush, HitBuff) runs its clock over its `duration`: nothing plays, but the impact, the VFX delays and `recovery` follow the phases (`swingStart` 0 and `strike` at the impact make it a plain speed-up). The `attack-inspect` skill measures the clips (the cocked pose and the strike), writes the values and films the attacks in `Tests/CombatSandbox`.

### Weapon trail

A sword artifact (`AbilityData.SwingsBlade`: `ArtifactData.weapon` sword or both) leaves a ribbon behind its blade during the swing: `AttackAnimationAction` asks the caster's `WeaponTrail` (on `Player.prefab`) for it over `AbilityData.SwingWindow` (from the end of the held pose to the strike as the clock plays them, plus 0.04 s; the last 0.2 s before the impact without a timing), in `AttackColor` (`playerColor`). Each frame of the swing keeps the blade's segment (`hilt` to `tip`, in the local space of the sword, `Epee`) for `lifetime` seconds, `subdivisions` slices added between two frames along a Catmull-Rom curve, in scaled time (a hit stop holds it). The mesh is built on a child object it creates, drawn by `Art/VFX/WeaponTrail/WeaponTrail.mat` (`WeaponTrail.shader`, additive: bright at the blade's edge and its tip, fading with each slice's age, thin streaks), in the artifact's hue at full value times `intensity`; a pale color (below `minSaturation`: white, gray) takes `defaultColor`, the outfit's blue, which additive white would blow out. It is an Anniversary addition, listed in the `EditionOnly` of `Player.prefab`.

### VFX

The VFX of the abilities and the hit VFX of `EntityFeedback` come from `VFXPool` (`Combat/VFX`): `Get` reactivates a released instance of the prefab and restarts its particle systems and VFX graphs, `Release` deactivates it under the pool object (instead of `Instantiate` and `Destroy`). `Get` takes the prefab the current [edition](editions.md) pairs with the one asked (`EditionSkin.Resolve`: the original's VFX in the Classic, when paired), and a new instance gets the edition's materials (`EditionMaterials.Apply`). The pool lives in the active scene and dies with it. Don't give a pooled VFX prefab a script that destroys it or keeps state between plays.

A `VFXInfo` of an ability plays a `prefab` after `delay` seconds, parented to its `target`: the player's markers (`GUN`, `SWORD`, `LEFTHAND`, `RIGHTHAND`, `BACK`, from `PlayerAttack.Anchor`), `SOURCETILE` or `TARGETTILE`; an enemy has no markers, so its body targets start from its tile. It faces the target tile, plus `rotationOffset` degrees (the caster's facing when it targets its own tile). The delay is a coroutine on the caster: a caster disabled or destroyed meanwhile never plays it.

A `ProjectileInfo` is the one VFX whose timing depends on the distance: at the strike, `Fly` takes its `prefab` from the pool at its `origin` (a `VFXInfo.Target`, through `VFXInfo.Origin`) plus a world `offset` (an enemy has no markers: the height of its shot above its tile), turned so that its local -Z faces the target, and moves it at `speed` units per second (a tile is 1 unit) to the nearest point of the body of the entity on the targeted tile (the bounds of its solid colliders), or to the tile's center at its height; its arrival ends the `AttackAnimationAction`, so the effects follow it. The flight runs on the `ActionManager` and the attack releases the projectile with its VFX. Its prefab is only a visual: no velocity or collision of its own, particles simulated in local space to follow its root, trails in world space (`worldSpace`) to stay behind, and a lifetime longer than the flight (`PrecisionShotBullet`).

The root of a VFX prefab stays at position 0, rotation 0 and scale 1: the code places it (`VFXInfo` sets its local position to the `offset` and its rotation towards the target through `ConstantRotation`, `ProjectileInfo` moves it, `EntityFeedback` and `EntityParticles` put it in the world without rotation), so a transform on the root would be dropped or, for the scale, applied without showing in the prefab. An effect that needs an offset, a tilt or a scale is a wrapper `<Name>.prefab`, referenced by the data, nesting its content `<Name>VFX.prefab` (itself with a clean root) whose instance carries that transform.

`VFXWarmup.Warm` plays VFX once out of sight before they show: it takes their instances from the pool, simulates them and renders them with a hidden camera (a 128 px texture far below the rooms), which compiles their shaders and fills the pool; the first hit of an effect would otherwise freeze the game. It is called by `Room.Init` (the patterns of the room's enemies, from `EnemyAI.AllPatterns`, the player's artifacts and the `EntityFeedback.hitVFX` of the room's entities and the player), by `Collectable.SetArtifacts` (a chest's artifacts, before the player can take them) and by `InventoryManager` when the grid changes. Each prefab is warmed once per scene, and again in the new edition by `VFXWarmup.Rewarm`, which `Edition.Set` calls behind the switch's wipe: the materials and the pipeline (its MSAA) changed, and so did the shaders to compile.

The spells' particle shaders are hand-written URP HLSL, unlit, in the transparent queue ("Towards the Unknown/VFX/..."): they read the particle systems' vertex streams (the color, uv0 with the custom data in zw, uv1, uv3) through `Rendering/VFX/VFXParticles.hlsl`. The center glow family (panning texture bent by a flow texture, noise, center glow shrinking with the custom data; added, blended, strong, or faded by a fresnel) shares `Rendering/VFX/CenterGlow.hlsl`, the dissolving one `Rendering/VFX/DissolveNoise.hlsl`; the others (DragonStrike's aura, ground wave and wind, WithoutFear's cutout, glow and distortion) sit next to their effect in `Art/VFX`. They were ported from Amplify with the same properties and keywords, so the materials kept their values. Soft particles are left out, as the Amplify shaders never faded under URP; WithoutFear's distortion reads the opaque texture (URP runs no GrabPass).

The player's attacks have their own Anniversary VFX in `Prefabs/VFX/Attacks` (the rework is tracked in [attack rework](../attack-rework.md)), paired in `ClassicSkin` with a copy of the prefab they replace (`Art/Classic/Prefabs/VFX/Attacks/<Name>_Classic`), which the Classic plays unchanged; the enemies keep the prefabs of `Prefabs/VFX`. A graph timed for the attack's former rhythm plays faster through `VFXPlayRate` (`playRate` on the graphs under it and a multiple of the particle systems' simulation speed, set on enable; a nested `VFXPlayRate` keeps what it holds at its own rate, as the impact sparks) so that it lands on the retimed impact.

Keep the capacity of a VFX graph's systems close to what it spawns: the buffers are allocated for the whole capacity when the effect is created (measured peaks: about 200 particles for the Kameiko claw slash, capacity 4096).

## Effects

Effects are `[SerializeReference]` subclasses of `CombatEffect` (`Combat/Effects/CombatEffect.cs`). They only queue actions, so everything plays in order; the instant ones queue a call (`ActionManager.AddToBottom(() => …)`):

| Effect | Queued | Description arguments |
|---|---|---|
| `DamageEffect` | Random damage between min and max, times the caster's dealt and the damaged entity's received multipliers (`EntityStats.DamageTo`); `ignoreArmor` sends it straight to the health (Explosive Sacrifice's self damage). On the caster it is `caster.DamageTo(caster, …)`: self damage grows with its own `AttackUp` and `DefenseDown`. `Ability.PreviewDamage`, behind the damage preview, only counts the effects on the target | `minDamage`, `maxDamage` (`minSelfDamage`, `maxSelfDamage` on the caster) |
| `ArmorEffect` | `GainArmor` | `armor` |
| `HealEffect` | `Heal` | `heal` |
| `StatModifierEffect` | `AddStatusEffect` with a `StatusEffectData` and a duration | `<status>Turns` |
| `MoveEffect` | `MoveTowardsAction`: moves one entity in a straight line towards (positive distance) or away from the other, along the axis where they are furthest apart (the z axis on a tie); it stops before the first tile that is missing, taken or not walkable, and a dead entity (killed by the same attack) is not moved | `distance` |

Each effect chooses its entity (`EffectTarget.Target` or `Caster`). Renaming or moving an effect class breaks the assets using it unless it gets a `[MovedFrom]` attribute.

## Damage and armor

`EntityStats.TakeDamage` removes the armor first, then the health, unless the damage ignores the armor. The armor resets at the start of the entity's turn and at the end of the combat. At 0 health, the entity raises `Died` and `GameEvents.EntityDied` (its death animation starts), leaves the board and the turn system and queues a `DieAction`. The action turns off its colliders and destroys it once `EntityFeedback.deathDuration` has passed since its death, without holding the queue.

## Status effects

Status effects are `StatusEffectData` assets (`Assets/Data/StatusEffects`: `AttackUp`, `AttackDown`, `DefenseUp`, `DefenseDown`):

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `stat` | `DamageDealt` or `DamageReceived` multiplier |
| `delta` | Added to the multiplier while the status lasts |
| `isBuff` | Shown as a buff or a debuff in the HUD |
| `opposite` | Applying the status on an entity having the opposite removes the opposite instead of adding it (`StatusApplied` is raised all the same) |

`EntityStats` keeps its active statuses by asset (applying one again extends it to the longest duration), counts their turns down at the start of its own turns (a status of 1 turn put on an enemy by the player ends as that enemy's turn starts: it only weighs on the rest of the player's turn), clears them at the end of the combat, and computes `DamageDealtMultiplier` / `DamageReceivedMultiplier` from its base values plus the active deltas. The status panel of the HUD reads the stat and the buff flag from the data.
