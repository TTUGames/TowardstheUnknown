# Entities

## Components

Each combatant GameObject combines:

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| `EntityStats` | The model: health, armor, damage multipliers, status effects; raises `StatsChanged`, `Hit`, `Died`. `OnDamageTaken(amount)`, called after the health drops and before the death check, lets a subclass react to a hit (Drareg stops at its phase threshold) |
| `EntityFeedback` (`Visuals`) | The hit VFX (`hitVFX`, set on the `Player`, `Enemy` and `Drareg` prefabs, `hitVFXHeight` above the entity's feet, facing away from the entity playing its turn as the recoil), the white flash when health is lost (`HitFlash`), the recoil of the model (see [hit feedback](#hit-feedback)) and the hit and death animations (`EntityAnimator.PlayHit`, `PlayDeath`), from the stats' events; `deathDuration`, how long the corpse stays for its death animation, the last `vanishDuration` of it shrinking into the ground, announced by the static `EntityFeedback.VanishStarted`; with the [edition](editions.md)'s `deathAnimation` off (the Classic), `DeathDuration` is 0 and the entity is removed at once, and `hitReactions` off leaves out the flash and the recoil |
| `EntityAnimator` (`Visuals`) | The only script driving the entity's `Animator`, see [animation](#animation) |
| `FootIK` (`Visuals`) | Plants a humanoid's feet on the ground (on `Player` and `Drareg`), see [foot IK](#foot-ik) |
| `GarmentSprings` (`Visuals`) | Swings the player's coat flaps and skirt, see [garments](#garments) |
| `EntityTurn` | Turn hooks (`OnTurnLaunch`, `OnTurnStop`, `OnCombatEnd`) |
| `TacticsMove` | Tile pathing and movement, through `MoveAction`; `CurrentTile` is the tile it stands on (`Tile.GetEntity()` gives back the entity's `TacticsMove`), `Stats` its `EntityStats`, which reach it back through `Move` and `Tile`; `SlideToTile` moves without walking at `slideSpeed` for the pushes, pulls and dashes of `MoveTowardsAction` (in the Classic [edition](editions.md), `slideMoves` off, they are walks, as the original's); walks and runs through `EntityAnimator.SetLocomotion` |
| `EnemyAttack` (enemies) | The enemy's runtime patterns: `TryAttack` casts the first one that can reach its target, `GetThreatenedTiles` the tiles its player-targeting patterns reach from every tile it can walk to (the enemy info's threat), found again only when `Tile.BoardVersion`, the enemy's tile, its movement points or its patterns changed: a hover or a hit shows the cached tiles. The player has no counterpart: `PlayerAttack` shows the aimed artifact's range and targets (`CheckAndPreviewArtifact`) |
| `EntityOutline` (`Visuals`, disabled) | Outlines the entity's meshes, seen through the walls (the timeline enables it on hover); drawn by the `OutlineFeature` of the URP renderer (silhouette mask, then a full-screen pass with `Rendering/Outline.shader`) without touching the materials |
| `EntityRing` (`Visuals`) | The ring under the entity during the deploy phase and the combat (`Rendering/EntityRing.shader`, `Mat_RingPlayer` blue, `Mat_RingEnemy` red, in `Art/Materials/Tiles`): a separate object following the entity, so that the outline, hit flash and dissolve leave it out. On the entity's turn in a combat (`TurnSystem.TurnChanged`, `IsCurrentTurn`; cleared at the combat's end) it brightens (`_ActiveBoost`), pulses (`_PulseSpeed`, `_PulseStrength`) and sends a thin echo outwards at each pulse's peak (`_Ripple`, over `_RippleReach`; the quad is 1.35 times the ring's `diameter` to leave it room). It brightens while hovered (`BoardPointer.EntityHovered`: its tile, its model or its timeline item, `_HoverBoost`): the shader takes the stronger of the turn's and the hover's boosts plus a part of the other, so that both show without burning out. It takes the target color while the selected artifact would hit it (`PlayerAttack.TargetsPreviewed`), the echo included, and fades out on death |
| `FootstepAudio` | Posts the footstep event from the walk animation events |
| `FootstepDust` | On the same `PlayFootstep` animation events, puffs a few dust particles on the ground under the planted foot (the lowest one: the humanoid foot bones, or the bones named `Foot` or `Hand` of a generic rig, found again when the avatar changes). It instantiates `Prefabs/VFX/FootstepDust.prefab` (world space, no emission of its own, local scaling so that the entity's scale, 0.3 for the player, does not shrink it, `FootstepDust.mat` alpha blended) as a child; `count` and `scale` size the puffs per prefab. The animation events reach it even disabled: it checks `enabled` (the Classic [edition](editions.md) disables it) |

## Animation

Every entity plays the same base controller, `Art/Animations/Animators/Entity.controller`, through an `AnimatorOverrideController` of its own next to it (`Player`, `Drareg`, `Golem`, `Nanuko` for the Nanuko and GreatNanuko, `Kameiko` for the Kameiko and GreatKameiko). The controller's states play placeholder clips, sub-assets of `Entity.controller` named `<state> Slot`; an override maps them to the entity's clips. Give a new entity an override rather than a controller. The clips sit in `Art/Animations` by rig: `Humanoid` (the player and Drareg: `Idle`, `Walk`, `Run`, `Death`, the `Hit*` clips, and `Abilities`, one clip per ability, for the attack clips of the ability data) and `Golem`; the Kameiko and Nanuko play the wolf and bear clips of their MalbersAnimations pack.

| Layer | States | Parameters |
|---|---|---|
| Locomotion | `Idle`, `Walk`, `Run` | bools `Walking`, `Running`; speeds `WalkSpeed`, `RunSpeed` |
| Action | `Empty`, `AttackA`, `AttackB` | normalized times (Motion Time) `AttackTimeA`, `AttackTimeB` |
| UpperAction (mask `UpperBody`) | the same states, the same slots | the same |
| Reaction | `Empty`, `HitNone`, `HitSmall`, `HitRegular`, `HitCritical`, `Death` | |

`EntityAnimator` (on `Player.prefab`, `Enemy.prefab` and `Drareg.prefab`) is the only script touching the `Animator`: the layers, states and parameters it names are the contract of `Entity.controller`, so rename them on both sides. In `Awake` it wraps the base controller in a runtime override and copies the entity's overrides into it, since each attack rewrites the clips of its slots. Every clip it plays goes through the [edition](editions.md)'s `EditionSkin.Current(clip)`: the entity's clips in `Awake` and on `Edition.Changed`, each attack's and follow-up's in `PlayAttack`, so that a clip the Anniversary changed plays the original's in the Classic once paired.

- The Action, UpperAction and Reaction layers weigh 0 in the controller and `EntityAnimator` fades their weight in and out: an `Empty` state at weight 1 would override a humanoid's pose. Don't set their default weight to 1.
- `PlayAttack(clip, speed, followUp, clock, legs)` plays the ability's clip (`AbilityData.animationClip`, see [abilities](combat.md#abilities)) in the slot `AttackA` or `AttackB` not played last, so that an attack blends into the next one, even the same clip: `attackFade` from the locomotion, `chainedAttackFade` while an attack still plays, `followUpFade` into the follow-up clip, and `attackFadeOut` back to the locomotion, ending with the last clip. An attack plays in both attack layers: Action, the whole body, at the weight of `legs` (`AbilityData.legs`), and UpperAction, masked to the upper body (`Art/Animations/Animators/UpperBody.mask`: all but the root, the legs and the feet's IK), at full weight while `legs` is under 1. The upper body plays the attack, the legs blend between the stance of the locomotion and the clip: 0 keeps them standing (no step, no planted foot sliding), which suits a spell cast from the spot; a generic rig, or an edition whose `attackLegs` is off (the Classic), plays the whole body. The attack states play at the time `EntityAnimator` sets at each frame (Motion Time, the parameters `AttackTimeA` and `AttackTimeB` of `Entity.controller`), from the attack's `AttackClock` (see [attack timing](combat.md#attack-timing); linear without one, a follow-up clip always is). A clip may run past its ability's `duration`: once the attack's action ended (`EndAttack`), the rest of the clip is its recovery, played out standing still and cut by a walk or a run (`SetLocomotion`, blending out over `attackFadeOut`); a move during the attack itself (a dash) does not cut it. The attack clips are serialized on the data, never named in the code; the slots' placeholder clips are listed in `attackSlots`.
- `PlayHit(healthLost)` picks `HitNone` when the armor took it all, `HitSmall`, `HitRegular` from `regularHitDamage` (25) and `HitCritical` from `criticalHitDamage` (40), then fades out with the clip. `PlayDeath` plays over everything, for good, and stops the other animations.
- `SetAvatar` changes the model's avatar (Drareg's second phase) and sets the speeds again, as the rebind resets the parameters.

The player's root motion is off: the tiles move the entities, not the clips.

Every state of `Entity.controller` has Unity's Foot IK on (`m_IKOnFeet`): a humanoid clip is retargeted muscle by muscle, which keeps angles, not the feet's places, so a clip made on another rig (the Mixamo rest pose of the player turns its feet out by 9° and its knees differ by 3°) slides the feet; Foot IK brings them back where the clip's own rig had them. The [foot IK](#foot-ik) then lays them on the ground.

### Foot IK

The clips don't keep the feet on the ground (the idle tilts them, toes in the ground and heels up; some attacks float them). `FootIK`, on the humanoids (`Player`, `Drareg`; it disables itself on a generic rig), plants them in `OnAnimatorIK`, which the IK pass of the four layers of `Entity.controller` calls: for each foot, a raycast on `ground` (the `Terrain` layer) under its animated position, then the foot laid flat on the surface (its sole at the avatar's `feetBottomHeight`, sunk by `sink`, following `slopeFollow` of the slope, keeping the direction the animation gives it). A foot is planted while its animated sole stays under `plantedHeight` above the character's feet, and free from `liftedHeight`: the steps of the walk, the run and the attacks keep their animation. The pelvis goes down (at most `maxPelvisOffset`, smoothed over `pelvisSmoothing`) so that the lower foot reaches the ground, and always `crouch` lower (1.4 cm on the player, about 2 % of its hips' height): the planted feet stay on the ground, so the knees bend a little and the character sits into it. Selected in Play mode, it draws the rays and the targets (green when planted); `Probe.Feet` of the playtest reads its state. It is an Anniversary addition, listed in the `EditionOnly` of both prefabs; turned off, it forgets its pelvis offset.

### Garments

The player's coat and skirt swing in the Anniversary. The rig has only Mixamo's bones, so the chains are added under the pelvis in `Player.prefab`: six per garment around the body (`Coat_FrontL_0` to `Coat_FrontR_End`, three bones and a tip; `Skirt_*`, two bones), starting on the garment's surface at the belt and ending at its hem. The meshes `Art/Models/Characters/Protagonist/Protagonist_Coat` and `Protagonist_Skirt` are copies of the FBX's `Veste` and `Jupe` whose vertices below the belt are handed over to the chains, more towards the hem, between the two nearest chains but never across the front opening; at the bind pose they match the originals. The hem is 85 to 90 % on the chains, the rest staying on the thighs. Their UV2.x holds the hem gradient (0 above the swing, 1 at the hem) for the [glow](#player). The renderers `Veste` (`GlowJacket`) and `Jupe` (`Art/VFX/GlowClothes/Skirt/GlowSkirt.mat`, a Character Glow variant on the skirt's texture, without bands) show them, listed in `PlayerGlow`'s outfit; `Veste_Classic` and `Jupe_Classic` keep the FBX's meshes for the Classic (`EditionOnly` of each edition on the prefab).

`GarmentSprings`, on the player's root, runs after the animation and the foot IK: each bone of a chain is set back to its rest under its parent, the chain's first bone turning with the thigh on its side (`Chain.follow` of the thigh's rotation in the pelvis's space since the bind pose, `thighRest`: the coat's front 85 %, sides 55 %, back 30 %, the skirt's more), so that a stepping leg carries the flaps in front of it instead of going through them; then its tail follows the spring (inertia less `drag`, a pull towards the rest direction of `stiffness`, the room's wind times `windResponse`, a `gravity`), kept at its length, its tip and its middle pushed out of the thighs' capsules (`colliders`, from `UpLeg` to `Leg`, the trousers' radius, plus `boneRadius`) and within `maxAngle` of the rest. Stylized rather than real: a low drag lets the flaps overshoot, their rest turns outwards by `flare` as if lifted, and they undulate slowly (`undulation`, `undulationSpeed`, each chain in its own phase, the wave running down the chain by `undulationLag`). The wind is `Wind.At` (see [wind](map.md#wind)): the breeze stirs the flaps, a passing gust lifts them. The skirt is the inner layer: each of its chains copies, bone for bone, the swing of the coat's chain at the same azimuth (`Chain.leader`) and is only pushed out of the legs, so that the two layers never cross. In scaled time: a hit stop freezes the cloth and a slow motion slows it. A move longer than `teleportDistance` in one frame (a room change) puts the chains back at rest; turned off, they go back to rest. Selected, it draws the chains and the capsules. The chains, the meshes and their wiring were made once from `Veste_Classic` and `Jupe_Classic` by a `run_script` of the `unity` CLI (not kept in the project): a change of the chains means making them again the same way.

## Hit feedback

A hit that takes health flashes the entity, freezes the time and shakes the camera; the flash and the shake run in unscaled time, so that they play through the hit stop:

- Flash (`EntityFeedback`: `flashStrength` 0.75, faded out over `flashDuration` 0.18 s), red in the Anniversary (the `flashColor` of its renderer's `OutlineFeature`), white in the Classic. `HitFlash` lists the entity's mesh and skinned renderers once; the `OutlineFeature` draws them again with the `Flash` pass of `Rendering/Outline.shader` (flat `flashColor` of the feature, alpha times the amount, depth tested so walls in front hide it). The entity's materials, keywords and property blocks are never touched, so the flash works the same on URP Lit, the dissolve Shader Graphs, `CharacterGlow` (whose `_GlowMultiplier` `PlayerGlow` keeps driving), `SpectralGlow` and `EnemyEnergy` (whose aura pass it leaves out), and nothing is left to restore. A hit the armor takes whole does not flash.
- Hit stop and camera shake (`ImpactFeedback` on `Managers/Gameplay.prefab`, all fields defaulted in code). `HitWeight` turns the health lost into 0..1: 1 health lost is 0, `heavyHitHealth` (40) or more is 1, multiplied by `playerHitWeight` (1.5) on the player; each hit's weight is raised as `ImpactFeedback.HitWeighed` (the wind's hit waves use it, see [wind](map.md#wind)). The hit stop goes from `lightHitStop` (0.035 s, about two frames) to `heavyHitStop` (0.09 s), a kill freezes `killHitStop` (0.12 s) and the last kill of a combat slows the time to `lastKillTimeScale` (0.25) for `lastKillDuration` (1 s), while the camera zooms in by `lastKillZoom` (18 % of the orthographic size) and moves `lastKillFocus` (40 %) of the way towards the kill, in over `lastKillZoomIn` and out over `lastKillZoomOut` once the slow motion ends (real time), and `lastKillSound` (the `Finisher` event) plays over the attack's. `ImpactFeedback` is the one writer of the camera's transform and orthographic size. The shake trauma goes from `lightHitTrauma` (0.15) to `heavyHitTrauma` (0.55), `blockedHitTrauma` (0.1) for a hit the armor takes, `killTrauma` (0.65) for a kill; hits and kills raise the trauma to their level rather than adding to it, so an area hitting several enemies shakes like its heaviest hit (a boss phase adds `bossPhaseTrauma`). The shake is the trauma squared times the screen shake setting (`GameSettings.ScreenShake`, 0 disables it), up to `maxOffset` and `maxRoll`, and fades at `recovery` per second. A hit taking health also jolts the camera along it, from the entity playing its turn (the attacker) to the one hit, on the screen plane (`Kick`): out at once, back eased over `kickDuration` (0.22 s, real time), from `lightKick` (3 cm) to `heavyKick` (10 cm) with the hit's weight, zooming in by up to `heavyKickZoom` (2.5 % of the view), times the screen shake setting.
- Blood (`BloodFeedback`, on `Managers/Gameplay.prefab`, Anniversary only): a hit taking health (`ImpactFeedback.HitWeighed`) throws a spurt from the middle of the body, away from the attacker and a little up (`rise`): `lightCount` to `heavyCount` jets (5 to 14) at `lightSpeed` to `heavySpeed` with the hit's weight. `Prefabs/VFX/BloodSpurt` draws them as streaks stretched along their velocity, the faster the longer (a stretched billboard of `Art/VFX/Blood/BloodStreak.png`, unlit `Mat_BloodJet` so that they keep their red at night), falling and gone on the terrain. As the spurt lands (`landDelay`), the code lays the marks on the ground itself, found by raycasts on `ground`, without particle collisions: a splash at the root and 2 to 5 streaks along the throw (`streaks`, `reach`, `streakLength`, `streakWidth`, `spread`), emitted into `Prefabs/VFX/BloodMarks` (instantiated once: horizontal billboards of the streak, lit, and its `Splashes` child of `BloodSplat.png`), which keep them 26 to 32 s and fade them out very slowly. The marks and the jets in flight are cleared when the player leaves the room (`GameEvents.RoomLeft`): the rooms share the same place in the world. An entity of `variants` throws something else and leaves no mark: the Golem, `Prefabs/VFX/CrystalShards` (small crystals of its `Golem_Crystal` material, spinning, bouncing, lying a few seconds, then shrinking away).
- Recoil (`EntityFeedback`): the model is pushed away from the entity playing its turn (the attacker; none on its own turn) at once, so that the hit stop holds the peak, then settles back over `recoilDuration` (0.25 s, game time). The push goes from `lightRecoil` (5 cm) to `heavyRecoil` (16 cm) with the hit's weight (`ImpactFeedback.HitWeighed`), `blockedRecoil` when the armor takes it all, and the model flattens and widens by `squashPerMeter` of the push (0.6), then swings back through a slight stretch, taller and thinner, before it settles (squash and stretch). It moves the entity's direct children holding a mesh, never the entity, which the tiles and moves place; the animation may rewrite their position or scale each frame, so each value only loses the offset the recoil wrote if it still holds it.
- Heals and armor (`RecoveryFeedback` on `Managers/Gameplay.prefab`, from `GameEvents.Healed` and `ArmorGained`): motes rise from the body (`DeathDissipation.prefab` again), in `healColor` or `armorColor` (the UI's `--color-heal` and `--color-shield`). `EntityParticles` plays a particle prefab on a body for both components: from the VFX pool, tinted, its shape sized by the body's mesh bounds, released once its particles are gone.
- Armor broken (`ArmorBreakFeedback` on `Managers/Gameplay.prefab`, from `GameEvents.DamageTaken` when the armor took part of a hit and none is left): glass shards burst from the body, tumble in 3D, fall and fade (`Prefabs/VFX/ArmorShatter.prefab`: quads of `Art/VFX/ArmorBreak/GlassShard.png`, a triangle bright along its edges, `GlassShard.mat` additive and double sided, plus a flash), in `shieldColor`.
- Deaths (`DeathFeedback` on `Managers/Gameplay.prefab`): an enemy's death bursts sparks and a flash from the middle of its body (`Prefabs/VFX/DeathBurst.prefab`), and motes rise from its corpse as it vanishes (`DeathDissipation.prefab`, on `EntityFeedback.VanishStarted`), sized by the body's bounds and tinted by its energy color, the `_GlowColor` of its materials (`fallbackColor` otherwise: the Golem's crystal). Their material, `Art/VFX/Death/DeathSpark.mat` (URP particles, additive, `BasicDamage/Glow.png`), holds the brightness in HDR: the particles' colors are clamped to 1.

## Turn camera focus

The camera stays fixed but for one short nudge when the side changes (`TurnCameraFocus` on `Managers/Gameplay.prefab`, from `TurnSystem.TurnChanged`): when the enemies' turns begin, it eases over `focusDuration` (0.6 s) towards the first enemy to act, by `strength` (0.15) of the distance from the middle of the screen to it, at most `maxShift` (0.35 m, a few percent of the orthographic view), holds for the rest of the enemies' turns, and eases back to its rest over `returnDuration` (0.45 s) on the player's turn, at the end of the combat or of the run; leaving the room snaps it back. It only computes an offset (`TurnCameraFocus.Offset`, in scaled time: it holds through the pause and the hit stops): `ImpactFeedback`, the one writer of the camera's transform, shakes around its rest plus that offset. Purely visual, it runs beside the gameplay and never waits for the action queue. The HUD elements placed over the entities once (popups, damage previews, enemy info panel) don't follow it, as with the shake, hence the small shift.

## Player

`Entities/Player.prefab` combines `PlayerStats` (energy, spent both to move and to cast; heals `antechamberHeal` on the first visit of an antechamber and `combatVictoryHeal` after each combat won; `IsHealthLow` under `LowHealthShare` of the maximum health), `PlayerTurn`, `PlayerMove`, `PlayerAttack`, `InventoryManager` (starting artifacts, see [Inventory](inventory.md)) and `PlayerHurtAudio` (the music muffled by the hits and the heartbeat at low health, see [audio](../tech/audio.md#mix)).

`PlayerTurn` is the controller. It enters one `IPlayerMode` at a time, `PlayerMove` or `PlayerAttack`, and forwards it the room's `TileHovered` / `TileClicked` events:

- `PlayerMove` shows the reachable tiles (all of them out of combat) and, in combat, the path to the hovered one (target highlight, and `PathPreviewed` with the tiles from the player's, drawn by `PathLine`, see [tiles](map.md#tiles)), and moves to the clicked one; out of combat, clicking while moving redirects the movement. Stopping on an exit out of combat calls `Map.MoveToAdjacentRoom`, and `IsMapTransitioning` keeps the reachable tiles hidden until `Map` has deployed the player in the next room. The reachable tiles are searched again at the end of each move while it is the current mode; an enemy searches its own once per turn, in `EnemyMove.MoveTowardsTarget`.
- `PlayerAttack` shows the range of the selected artifact and, under the pointer, its targets (again when another artifact is selected); clicking casts it, then goes back to moving once the actions are done (see [cast queue](#cast-queue)). Pointing at an entity's model or at its timeline item counts as pointing at its tile, so clicking either casts on it.
- The number keys and the skills bar select an artifact (`SetState(ATTACK, index)`); the key or the skill of the artifact already aimed with goes back to moving, as `Cancel` does, and a key raises `SkillKeyPressed` for the bar's click sound. `Cancel` also drops the queued casts. Nothing reacts outside the player's combat turn, while a menu is open or while the action queue is busy, but for aiming and queuing during the player's own casts.
- A click that does nothing during the player's combat turn (an empty floor tile out of reach, a tile out of the aimed artifact's range or that it can't target) blinks the tile red and raises `ClickRefused` (`PlayerTurn.RefuseClick`, see [tiles](map.md#tiles)).
- The player leaves its mode at the end of its turn and on `GameEvents.RoomLeft` (`SelectedArtifactChanged(-1)` if it was aiming), and enters the move mode at the start of each turn.

### Cast queue

In an edition with `EditionProfile.castQueue` (the Anniversary; the Classic waits for the cast to end, see [editions](editions.md)), while a cast plays (`PlayerAttack.IsCasting`, a whole chain of casts), the player can still select an artifact and click a target: the cast is paid at once (`Artifact.Pay`: energy, use, cooldown) and queued as a `QueuedCast` (artifact, tile, and the clicked entity for a single target, followed if it moves; an area keeps its tile). When the queue frees, `OnCastEnd` launches the next queued cast if it is still valid (its target alive, `CanTarget`, `CanReach` from where the player now stands, the combat still going with enemies left), else refunds it (`Artifact.Refund`, `PlayerStats.RefundEnergy`) and, if the combat and the player's turn still go on, raises `ArtifactRefused` (a chain cut by the last kill is refunded silently). Each chained cast cuts the recovery of the previous one to `chainedRecovery` (0.1 s, see [abilities](combat.md#abilities)).

- At the end of the chain, the artifact aimed meanwhile keeps its aim, its range computed from the player's new tile; otherwise the player goes back to moving.
- `CancelQueuedCasts` drops the queue and refunds it: on `Cancel`, at the end of the turn, on `RoomLeft`, at the end of the combat, and on `Edition.Changed` to an edition without the cast queue. A refund only undoes the cooldown if that payment started it.
- `TurnSystem.EndPlayerTurn` asks `PlayerAttack.DeferEndTurn` first: during a chain, the turn ends once the last cast is done; during a walk (the action queue busy), once the queue is free (`ActionManager.WhenFree`).
- `QueueChanged` (a cast queued, launched or dropped) refreshes the skills bar's counters and the markers over the targets (see [HUD](ui.md#hud)).

The player holds the sword, in combat or not. `Dissolving` and `PlayerGlow` show the artifact's weapon and neon color while casting (the gun, both, or empty hands for a bare-handed artifact; the enemies' attacks don't touch them), and `PlayerAttack` ends them once the chain of casts is over (`OnCastEnd`): the color and the gun's aim go, and the sword comes back (`Dissolving.HoldSword`) once the clip has played out (`EntityAnimator.IsAttacking`), so that a bare-handed recovery keeps its hands empty; a move cutting the clip brings it back at once. In both editions (`dissolveSpeed` in units per second; the blade grows over about 5.5 units, so the edition's `weaponAppearSpeed` draws it whole before the strike: 24 in the Anniversary, the original's 3 in the Classic, whole after about 2 s). Both go through property blocks of the weapons' renderers (`_DissolvePosition`, `_GlowColor`), never material instances, so that the edition swaps the weapons' shared materials: `PlayerGlow` sets the color as the material would take it (a block converts a color to linear, which the Classic's HDR property takes as it is: `SetVector` there). With the edition's `weaponSwapSpeed` (the Anniversary), a weapon drawn (`Undissolve`, and the sword coming back after the casts, `HoldSword`) first takes the other away at that speed and appears once it is gone: the sword and the gun never show together, but for an artifact wielding both. An artifact without a weapon (`none`) takes away the weapons still drawn, at the swap speed: it is cast bare-handed.


The glowing parts of the outfit (boots, jacket, mask: `Art/VFX/GlowClothes`) use `Rendering/CharacterGlow.shader` ("Towards the Unknown/Character Glow"): a lit PBR surface whose glow mask (red channel) emits `_GlowColor` at `_GlowIntensity` stops, with a rim of the glow color and a slow pulse; `_GlowMultiplier` is driven by the code. The glow runs like magic: waves climb the body in world space (`_FlowScale`, `_FlowSpeed`), their crests surging softly through the bands (`_SurgeStrength`), a fine sparkle (`_SparkleStrength`), a halo around the bands from the mask's blurred mips (`_HaloStrength`, `_HaloBlur`), the waves going from the glow color to a deeper shade of it (`_GradientShade`; `_HueShift` can turn that shade's hue, 0 keeps the whole outfit in one blue). A garment whose mesh carries the hem gradient (UV2.x, the [garments](#garments)) lights up towards its hem (`_HemGlow`, `_HemHeight`) and its fabric takes the glow's tint there (`_HemTint`). The fabric is matte (metallic 0, smoothness 0.12) with a grain of threads in its albedo and relief (`_FabricScale`, `_FabricGrain`, `_FabricBump`; the relief bends the normal through the screen-space slope of a height, without tangents), faded out where the threads would be finer than the pixels. The outfit is worn by the cold: its colors are washed out towards a pale cold gray (`_Weathering`), and snow lies on it in grainy patches (`_SnowScale` patches per meter, from `_SnowThreshold` over `_SnowSoftness`, `_SnowColor`, `_SnowSmoothness`, a relief of `_SnowBump`) that dim the bands under them, plus a few flakes. Neither the grain nor the snow read the UVs (the trousers, gloves and skirt have none): the washing out and the snow come from `Rendering/Weather.hlsl`, shared with the enemies' Enemy Energy, and each outfit mesh carries a weather channel (UV set 2, a Vector4): xyz its vertex at the bind pose, in meters, where the noises are drawn, fixed on the fabric whatever the animation and continuous from one part to the next; w how much snow settles there, from the prefab's pose: the head and shoulders the most, the back, the hem (UV2.x), a little everywhere, the front the least. The outfit's FBX meshes are copies holding it (`Art/Models/Characters/Protagonist/Protagonist_<mesh>.asset`, the same vertices, drawn in both editions, the Classic's Lit shader ignoring the channel), written by the same one-off script as the garments. In the Anniversary the whole outfit is Character Glow: besides the glowing parts, the belt, body, gloves, trousers, top and scarf materials (`Art/Models/Characters/Protagonist/Materials`) are variants without bands nor rim; their Lit originals are the Classic's (`Art/Classic/Models/Characters/Protagonist/Materials/*_Classic`, paired in `ClassicSkin`). `CharacterGlow.mat` holds every value; the variants only their albedo, mask, hem and weather (the jacket the most worn, the body the least). Their materials are variants of `GlowClothes/CharacterGlow.mat`, which holds the color (a pure blue, `_GlowColor` 0.09, 0.32, 1), the intensity, the rim and the pulse; the variants only set their albedo and mask. Both glow shaders are built from `Rendering/Glow.hlsl`, Character Glow with its glow mask define (the albedo map, the mask and the rim). `PlayerGlow` sets the outfit through property blocks on its shared materials, so that the [edition](editions.md) can swap them (the Classic's outfit is the original's `VFX_GlowGun` Shader Graph: color in `_LaserColor`, the neons at the original's rest blue times 3.5, no glow level; `EditionProfile.outfitColorProperty`, `neonRestColor`, `neonIntensity`, `outfitGlowLevel`), and starts over from the new materials on `Edition.Changed` (the Classic's instances go, a renderer still wearing one getting back the material it was made from: the Anniversary's skirt, whose `GlowSkirt` has no Classic pair, which `EditionMaterials` would not give back). The outfit's blue is the material's (the weapons get it times `intensity`, HDR); in the Classic, a cast tints them towards the artifact's color and back (`EditionProfile.castTint`). It sets the outfit's `_GlowMultiplier`, eased: 1 in exploration, from `emptyEnergyGlow` to 1 with the energy left during the player's turn, `enemyTurnGlow` during the enemies' turns, plus a `castFlash` fading over `flashDuration` when casting. It recomputes its level on the events (`EnergyChanged`, `TurnSystem.TurnChanged`, combat and exploration starts) and only updates while the glow moves.

### Weapons

The sword (`Art/VFX/CelestialSword/Sword.fbx`) and the gun (`Art/Models/Weapons/Pistolet.fbx`) hang from the first bone of an index finger in `Player.prefab`, as the original's, so that they turned with the fingers of each clip. In the Anniversary, `WeaponHold` (on the player's root, listed in its `EditionOnly`) puts them back on the hand's bone after the animation, at a fixed grip in the hand's space (`swordGrip`: the pose the finger gave the sword at the strike of BasicDamage, which the sword attacks were tuned on; `gunGrip`: the barrel along the hand, the grip in the palm, its top on the thumb's side; `gunRightGrip`, its mirror, for an artifact whose `ArtifactData.gunInRightHand` is set: PrecisionShot, whose clip shoots with the right hand). Its `DefaultExecutionOrder` puts it before the `WeaponTrail`. Turned off (the Classic), it gives the weapons their place under the finger back.

A gun shot aims: `PlayerAttack.Cast` passes `WeaponHold.Aim` the tile and `Artifact.StrikeDelay`, the real seconds to the shot as the edition's clock plays it. From `aimLead + aimBlend` (0.2 + 0.12 s) before the shot, the three spine bones (`spine`) turn, each taking its share, so that the barrel (`barrel`, the gun's -X) points from the `muzzle` (`GunMarker`) to the point the projectile flies to (`ProjectileInfo.Body`, the target's colliders, else the tile at the muzzle's height), and let go over `release` (0.06 s) after the shot, for the recoil; a clip pointing the gun more than `aimAngle` (80°) away is followed less, left as animated from `freeAngle` (120°: drawing the gun, OrbitalShot's shot at the sky). The spine and not the arm: an attack whose `legs` keep the stance loses the turn of the clip's hips, which put GunShot's clip 55° beside its target.

The weapons' Anniversary materials (`Art/Models/Weapons/Materials/Sword.mat`, `Gun.mat`) use `Rendering/WeaponGlow.shader` ("Towards the Unknown/Weapon Glow"): the outfit's Character Glow (`Glow.hlsl` with `GLOW_MASK` and `GLOW_DISSOLVE`: lit, washed out, its rim and its magic in the glow mask's bands, the mask read by its brightest channel since the weapons' are cyan), its albedo brightened (`_AlbedoBoost`: their textures are near black) and not very metallic, so that the ambient light shows them; drawn up to `_DissolvePosition` of the mesh's height (`Rendering/Dissolve.hlsl`, clipped in the shadow, depth and depth normals passes too), the cut glowing (`_DissolveGlow`). `PlayerGlow.intensity` is 1: the materials' `_GlowIntensity` holds the brightness, as the outfit's. The original's dissolve Shader Graphs and materials are the Classic's (`Art/Classic/Models/Weapons/Materials/Sword_Classic.mat`, `Gun_Classic.mat`, paired in `ClassicSkin`).

## Enemies

Every standard enemy prefab is a variant of `Entities/Enemies/Enemy.prefab` (GreatNanuko through `Nanuko.prefab`), which holds the shared components, layer, tag, the `TileWatcher` child (the point `TacticsMove.SetCurrentTileFromRaycast` casts down from onto its `terrainLayers`, both serialized) and the `Hover` child (a trigger box over the model, see [tiles](map.md#tiles); the Golem overrides its size); a variant adds its model and overrides its values (health, movement points, hit VFX height, `EntityData`). Create new enemies as variants of it. References to enemies point to their `EnemyAI` component (the `prefab` of a `RoomLayout` enemy placement).

The enemies are animals of the rift with a glow: the standard ones gray (the wolves) and light brown (the bears) with touches of blue, lightened by the snow on them, the Great ones black with touches of red and fragments of light rising from them. The touches are the bears' marks, a few drifting patches of veins and a faint rim. The Golem is carved in the caves' crystals: `Golem_Crystal`, a variant of the crystals' `Mat_MagicCrystal` (`Rendering/MagicCrystal.shader`) with its lengths at the model's scale (x16). The standard wolf's ice (its submesh 0: a foreleg, a haunch, the tail, the top of the head) is of the same crystal: `Kameiko_Energy`, another variant of `Mat_MagicCrystal`, at the wolf's scale (object units are meters, y up: `_GradientHeight` 0.9, `_VeinScale` 8, `_VeinDepth` 0.04), a little brighter (`_GlowStrength` 1) and only frosted (`_SnowOpacity` 0.25), so that it stands out of the gray body; `EnemyGlow` doesn't drive it (Magic Crystal has no `_GlowMultiplier`). The rest of the wolves and bears use `Rendering/EnemyEnergy.shader` ("Towards the Unknown/Enemy Energy", code in `EnemyEnergy.hlsl`):

- A lit surface (albedo map and tint, normal map, metallic, smoothness) whose veins of energy (ridges of a domain-warped value noise, `_VeinScale` per meter, `_VeinSharpness`, `_VeinAmount`, only where a slow low-frequency noise lets them surface, `_VeinCoverage`) stick to the body and crawl up it (`_VeinFlow`), brighter on the waves of a pulse travelling up (`_PulseSpeed`, `_PulseFrequency` per meter of height); the flesh burns darker around them (`_VeinDarken`). A glow mask (red channel) lights marks whole (the bears' `ShapeBear`), and a Fresnel rim outlines the silhouette. Lengths are in meters whatever the model's import scale (the noise runs on the object position times the object's scale), and the flow follows the world's up whichever axis the model stands on (whichever axis the model was built along).
- An aura: a second pass (`Aura`, light mode `SRPDefaultUnlit`, additive, no depth written) pushes a shell out along the normals by `_AuraWidth` meters and lights it in a band around the silhouette, eaten by flames of noise rising in world space (`_AuraNoiseScale`, `_AuraSpeed`, `_AuraCoverage`). A material without the aura lists that light mode in its `disabledShaderPasses` (saved in the `.mat`, nothing sets it at runtime): only `GreatKameiko_Flesh` and `GreatNanuko_Fur` keep it, so that a body gets one aura. The shader's queue is `Geometry+475`, after the opaque board, which would otherwise cover the aura.
- The energy is in stops (`_GlowIntensity`, `_AuraIntensity`) times `_GlowMultiplier`, driven at runtime.
- The cold marks them as the player's outfit (`Rendering/Weather.hlsl`, shared with Character Glow): their colors washed out (`_Weathering`) and grainy snow patches (`_SnowScale`, `_SnowThreshold`, `_SnowSoftness`, `_SnowColor`, `_SnowSmoothness`, a relief of `_SnowBump`, at `_SnowOpacity`: 85 %) dimming the veins and the marks under them. The snow is drawn on the weather channel of their meshes (UV set 2): `Art/Models/Characters/Kameiko/Kameiko_Weather` and `Nanuko/Nanuko_Weather`, copies of the FBX meshes (the same vertices, drawn in both editions) set on `Kameiko`, `GreatKameiko` and `Nanuko` (the Great bear and the dummy inherit them), and `Golem/Golem_Weather` on the `Golem`, holding each vertex at the bind pose and how much snow settles there for an animal: everywhere, the most on the back and the top of the head (the faces turned up in the prefab's pose), fading slowly down the flanks, with no line where it thins. Written once by a `run_script` of the `unity` CLI, as the outfit's. The Golem gets the same snow through Magic Crystal (`_SnowThreshold`, `_SnowSoftness`, `_SnowScale`, `_SnowColor`, `_SnowSmoothness`, `_SnowBump`, `_SnowOpacity`), which hides its glow under it; the cave's crystals, whose meshes have no weather channel, get none. Drareg has none.

Their materials are in `Art/Materials/Enemies`, variants of `EnemyEnergy.mat`: `Kameiko_Flesh` (submesh 1; `Kameiko_Energy` on submesh 0 is the crystal above), `Nanuko_Fur` (submesh 0) and `Nanuko_Marks` (submeshes 1 and 2, the `ShapeBear` mask), plain gray for the wolf and light brown for the bear, and the Great variants, black (the Malbers `Bear Black` texture for the bear), red, brighter, the only ones with the aura (the others disable its pass). The Malbers wolf textures don't match the UVs of the wolf model: its colors are plain. The bears' eyes (submeshes 3 and 4) use `EnemyEyes` and `EnemyEyes2`, Spectral Glow variants lit all over, and their red variants `EnemyEyes_Great` and `EnemyEyes_Great2`: two copies each, because the original model had a different material in each slot, which the Classic [edition](editions.md) gives back.

`EnemyGlow` (on `Enemy.prefab`) drives `_GlowMultiplier` through a property block of the model's renderers, eased: `turnGlow` on its turn in a combat, plus `hoverGlow` while hovered and `targetedGlow` while the selected artifact would hit it, a `hitFlare` fading over `hitFlareDuration` when hit (times the health lost over `heavyHitHealth`), an irregular flicker under `lowHealth` of its health, and 0 over `deathFade` when it dies. Its `wisps`, the particles of energy escaping from the body, follow the same level: their emission rate scales with it, and stops on death. Turned off (the Classic [edition](editions.md) disables it with `EntityRing` and `FootstepDust`), it stops and clears the wisps, clears its property blocks, and plays the wisps again once back on. The Great enemies have them: `Prefabs/VFX/EnemyWisps.prefab` (not played on awake: `EnemyGlow` plays them, so that they stay off while it is), a particle system emitting a few fragments of light from the body's skinned mesh (its shape's renderer set by each enemy prefab), rising slowly, drawn like the relics' fragments (`EnemyMote.mat`, `Rendering/RelicMote.shader`, tinted red by the particle color).

Drareg's marks use `Rendering/SpectralGlow.shader` ("Towards the Unknown/Spectral Glow"): a plain grey lit surface whose silhouette emits (a Fresnel rim, the whole surface with a `_RimPower` of 0) at `_GlowIntensity` stops, with a slow pulse and a `_GlowMultiplier`, with `GlowBlue2` (`Art/VFX/GlowEffect/BlueGlow`, lit all over, steady and brighter), a variant of `EnemyGlow.mat`, which holds the color. A crystal of the boss room keeps an environment glow material on the same shader (`PlantGlowWhite`); the glowing plants use Nature Lit (see [vegetation](map.md#vegetation)).

`Tests/EnemyShowcase` shows the enemies: the rig and `Map_EnemyShowcase` (see [architecture](../tech/architecture.md)), a combat against all of them, to tune their look and play. `Tests/CombatSandbox` sets a `TrainingDummy` against the player: a variant of `Kameiko` with its own `EntityData` (`TrainingDummy`, localized), whose `EntityStats.immortal` keeps it at 1 health at least and heals it back to full at the start of its turns, and whose `EnemyAI.passive` ends its turns without moving nor attacking.

`EnemyAI` is configured with an `EnemyPatternSet`: the distance to keep from the target (`targetDistance`) and an ordered list of `EnemyPatternData`. The first one drives the movement, the first usable one is cast: one whose `target` is `PLAYER` (the default) when the player is in its range (`EnemyPattern.CanTarget`), one whose `target` is `ENEMY` (`OnSelf`, the Great enemies' fluids) always, on the enemy's own tile: a buff on itself, left out of the threatened tiles. Its turn is an async method: `PlaySteps` moves, attacks, then ends the turn, awaiting `WaitForActions` between the steps, which stops the turn if the enemy died or the turn or combat ended meanwhile.

`EnemyMove.MoveTowardsTarget` first picks an objective tile, ignoring the movement points: among the empty tiles of the first pattern's range around the target, the ones whose distance is closest to `targetDistance`, the nearest to the enemy (the target's tile if there is none). It then scores each tile it can reach this turn by minus its walking distance to that objective, plus `canAttackBonus` (4) in the first pattern's range of the target, or else `canHideBonus` (2) out of the target's line of sight, and goes to the objective itself if it can reach it. Its own collider is off meanwhile, so that it doesn't block the lines of sight from where it would stand.

The HUD's `EntityInfoPanel` shows the hovered enemy's name, health and movement points, following `StatsChanged` (see [UI](ui.md#hud)); `ThreatTiles` marks its threatened tiles (see [map](map.md)).

## Drareg

Drareg, the boss (`Entities/Enemies/Drareg.prefab`, not a variant), uses subclasses, `DraregAI` and `DraregStats`, with the standard `EnemyAttack`, plus `DraregArena` on the boss room's root (it switches the two phases' decor, `firstPhaseDecor` and `secondPhaseDecor`) and `DraregPhaseTransitionAction`; both drive their shaders' `AppearProgress` through property blocks, never material instances.

- First phase: one of the `firstPhaseLayouts` pattern sets, picked randomly.
- At `phaseTransitionThreshold` health (`DraregStats.OnDamageTaken`), its health stops there and it switches to the second phase: `DraregAI.SwitchToSecondPhase` plays `chainedClip` as an attack at once, during the hit, and queues `DraregPhaseTransitionAction`, whose `OnStart` spawns the chains and then the orb (`transition` settings), which hides the switch of the model and its avatar (`EntityAnimator.SetAvatar`), `DraregArena` switches the room's decor (the background sphere's shader runs on a material instance, never on the asset), and `GameEvents.BossPhaseChanged(2)` changes the music.
- Second phase: the `secondPhase` pattern set, `secondPhaseMovementPoints`, and an ultimate: at the end of each of its turns, Drareg shows the indicator of the countdown (`ultimateCountdownIndicators`, by remaining turns), then counts it down; at 0 its next turn is the ultimate, and the countdown starts again from `ultimateCooldown`. The ultimate thus comes every `ultimateCooldown` + 1 turns of Drareg, the first on its turn `firstUltimateCooldown` + 1 after the switch (3 and 1 on the prefab: its second turn, then every fourth). It is cast as `ultimateSuccess` or `ultimateFail` depending on whether it reaches the player.
- Its death ends the run as a victory.

The second phase's model (`DraregPhase2Model.prefab`) feeds its VFX graph the body's shape through `SkinnedMeshToMesh`, which bakes the skinned mesh every `refreshRate` seconds (0.2) into a mesh for the graph's `Mesh` property. It keeps two meshes, the pose baked and its vertices alone, refilled at each refresh and destroyed with it.
